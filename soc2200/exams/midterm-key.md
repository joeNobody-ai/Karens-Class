# SOC 2200 — Midterm Answer Key

**Do not distribute to students.**

## Part A — Short answers (5 × 12)

### A1 — race as a social construction (12)

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | A correct definition of race as a category that sorts human bodies by real or imagined physical difference and treats the groups as facts of nature (Omi and Winant), plus a correct statement that "social construction" means made by people yet experienced as natural. |
| 4 | The essentialism/constructionism distinction stated accurately: essentialism posits a fixed inner nature; constructionism holds the category's meaning is produced and changeable. |
| 4 | The title echo used as *evidence of construction, not biology*: two titles, four decades apart, naming the same community's differences differently (documented: S1E1 *Reconcilable Differences*, 1987-09-24; revival *Irreconcilable Differences*). |

**Common errors:** asserting "race isn't real" without the second half (consequences are real); treating
the titles as the *cause* of anything; inventing plot beats not in the published summary.

### A2 — the racial project (12)

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | Omi and Winant's definition: an interpretation/representation of a racial category *linked to* an institutional arrangement. |
| 4 | Both halves made concrete from the documented summary (Kojo caught up in African/diaspora identity arguments in Ep 7): a named *meaning* (e.g. "authentic Blackness = X") and a named *arrangement* (e.g. the talent show's stage and its judging). |
| 4 | The link stated as a link, not two unrelated facts. |

**Common errors:** defining the racial project as just "an opinion about race"; leaving the arrangement
half blank; quoting dialogue that is not in the summary.

### A3 — prejudice, discrimination, institutional racism (12)

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | Three definitions kept distinct: prejudice = attitude/feeling; discrimination = action; institutional racism = inequality built into an organisation's routine practices regardless of intent. |
| 4 | A defensible placement of the documented summary (Deborah speaks her mind and is called out in Ep 2) at the level of individual action, with the reasoning stated. |
| 4 | A correct statement of the evidence that would move the claim up a level (e.g. a rule or practice, applied across cases, producing a racial pattern). |

**Common errors:** collapsing all three into "racism"; grading the characters' hearts instead of tracing
a mechanism; claiming institutional racism without naming any institution.

### A4 — the racial state (12)

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | Goldberg's definition: the state is structured *by* race, not a neutral umpire over it. |
| 4 | The mechanism by which a budget is racial without naming race: allocations fall unequally on groups already unequally positioned, so a colour-blind rule has colour-laden effects (documented: Ep 9 budget crisis; students organize). |
| 4 | An explicit statement that intent is not required for the outcome to be racial. |

**Common errors:** saying "the college is racist" as a verdict rather than tracing the allocation;
confusing the state with a person.

### A5 — colourism (12)

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | Hunter's definition: a hierarchy ranking people *within* a racial group by skin tone, usually privileging lighter tones. |
| 4 | The pageant standard (documented: Hazel runs for Miss Gilbert Hall, Ep 7) identified as a mechanism that ranks group members against each other. |
| 4 | The key insight stated: this is *intraracial* — it cannot be seen by a model that only has one group against society. |

**Common errors:** treating colourism as racism against the whole group; describing the pageant without
naming the *standard* it enforces.

## Part B — Applied essay (40)

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 8 | Documented facts stated and sourced (episode title and, for originals, air date), kept visibly separate from interpretation. |
| 12 | At least three concepts from Modules 1–7, each doing *distinct* work on a specific moment (no decoration, no synonym-stacking). |
| 8 | A genuine argument: a claim about the scene, not a summary of it. |
| 6 | Structure and agency (or race-as-made vs. race-as-imposed) identified in the scene. |
| 6 | An honest limit named: what the scene cannot show, and what a further source would be needed to establish. |

**Common errors:** essay that summarises the plot; three concepts all saying the same thing; an
interpretation presented as a documented fact; no limit stated.

**Marker's note:** the automatic rewrite applies here as elsewhere — an interpretation stated as fact
without the tier being marked is returned once, ungraded, with the rewrite condition named.
