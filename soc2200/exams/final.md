Two rules govern everything you write:

## Part A — Short answers (5 × 12 points = 60)

Answer each in a short paragraph. Define the concept, then apply it to the documented episode fact
named in the question.

**A1.** Define *racial formation* (Omi and Winant). The published summary of Ep 10, *A Gift & The
Curse*, states that major decisions are made about the college's future. Explain how a decision about
an institution is also a moment of racial formation — a category being made, inhabited, or remade.

**A2.** Define *intersectionality* (Crenshaw). The published summary of Ep 7 states that Hazel runs for
Miss Gilbert Hall and that the students deal with sexual health and relationships. Show why a
single-axis analysis (race only, or gender only) fails to capture what that summary points to.

**A3.** Define *the racial wealth gap* (Oliver and Shapiro) and explain the difference between *wealth*
and *income*. The published summary of Ep 9, *Romancing the Unknown*, states that Hillman's budget
crisis erupts. Which of the two — wealth or income — does a budget crisis most directly threaten, and
why does the distinction matter?

**A4.** Define *mass incarceration* (Alexander) and *criminalization*. The published summary of
*Honeymoon in L.A. (part 1)* (S6E120, first aired 1992-09-24) places a honeymoon against the 1992 Los
Angeles uprising. Explain what a family sitcom does, sociologically, by carrying that backdrop on
screen.

**A5.** Define *the boundaries of Blackness* as a form of *category work*. The published summary of Ep
7 states that Kojo is caught up in arguments over African and diaspora identity. Who, in that argument,
holds the authority to rule on belonging — and what is at stake in the ruling?
