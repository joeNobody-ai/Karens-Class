# SOC 2200 — Race and Ethnicity in American Society — Midterm

**Released:** after Module 7 · **Due:** before Module 9 · **Weight:** 20% of the final grade · **Format:** take-home, open-book · **Total:** 100 points

## Instructions

Answer every question. This is open-book: you may consult the modules, the readings, and the reference
site (https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/).

Two rules govern everything you write:

1. **The evidence rule.** Keep three kinds of claim apart: *documented* (an episode title, air date,
   credit, or published summary), *interpretation* (your reading, labelled as such), and *hypothesis* (a
   testable claim). Do not invent dialogue, quotes, plot beats, or statistics. Where you rely on a
   summary, say "the published summary states."
2. **AI note.** You may use AI to check grammar, but every claim you submit must be yours and sourced.
   A fabricated or hallucinated source is an academic-integrity violation however it was produced.

## Part A — Short answers (5 × 12 points = 60)

Answer each in a short paragraph. Define the concept, then apply it to the documented episode fact
named in the question.

**A1.** Define *race as a social construction*, and state the difference between *essentialism* and
*constructionism*. Then apply it: S1E1 is titled *Reconcilable Differences* (first aired 1987-09-24),
while the 2026 revival opens with *Irreconcilable Differences*. What does that echo suggest about race
as something made rather than fixed?

**A2.** Define Omi and Winant's *racial project*. The published summary of Ep 7, *Sometimes You Get the
Culture...Sometimes the Culture Gets You*, states that Kojo is caught up in arguments over African and
diaspora identity. Construct the racial project at work in that argument: which *meaning* is being
linked to which *arrangement*?

**A3.** Distinguish *prejudice*, *discrimination*, and *institutional racism*. The published summary of
Ep 2, *O Captain! My Captain!*, states that Deborah speaks her mind in public and is called out. Which
of the three terms best fits that summary, and what further evidence would you need to move the claim
up a level?

**A4.** Define Goldberg's *racial state*. The published summary of Ep 9, *Romancing the Unknown*,
states that Hillman's budget crisis erupts and students organize. Explain how a budget can be a racial
document even when the word "race" never appears in it.

**A5.** Define *colourism* (Hunter). The published summary of Ep 7 states that Hazel runs for Miss
Gilbert Hall. Explain how a pageant standard ranks people *inside* a group — and what that shows that a
model of a single group-versus-society line would miss.

## Part B — Applied essay (40 points)

Choose **one** screened scene — the talent-show argument in Ep 7, the budget crisis in Ep 9, or
*Honeymoon in L.A. (part 1)* (S6E120, first aired 1992-09-24) — and analyse it using **at least three
concepts from Modules 1–7**. Your essay must (a) state the documented facts you are using and name
their source; (b) keep each concept doing distinct work; and (c) separate what the scene shows from
what you are arguing it means.
