## Part A — Short answers (5 × 12)

### A1 — racial formation (12)

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | Omi and Winant's definition: the sociohistorical process by which racial categories are created, inhabited, transformed, and destroyed. |
| 4 | The link made concrete: an institutional decision (documented: Ep 10, *A Gift & The Curse* — major decisions about the college's future) reshapes who the college serves and who belongs, which is category-making, not just administration. |
| 4 | At least one of "made / inhabited / remade" explicitly matched to the decision. |

**Common errors:** treating racial formation as a synonym for "racism"; narrating the finale's plot
instead of analysing the decision as a category-making act.

### A2 — intersectionality (12)

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | Crenshaw's definition: overlapping identities produce experiences of advantage/disadvantage that cannot be understood by adding single categories together. |
| 4 | The "single-axis fails" point demonstrated on the documented summary (Miss Gilbert Hall pageant *and* sexual-health/relationship material in Ep 7), not asserted in the abstract. |
| 4 | A concrete statement of what a race-only and a gender-only reading each miss. |

**Common errors:** defining intersectionality as "everything is connected" (vacuous); listing categories
without showing the fusion.

### A3 — racial wealth gap / wealth vs. income (12)

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | Oliver and Shapiro's definition, plus the correct wealth/income distinction (stock vs. flow; what you own and can pass on vs. what you earn). |
| 4 | A defensible answer on which the budget crisis threatens: it most directly hits the *flow* (tuition, income to stay enrolled) but cascades into the *stock* (who can draw on family wealth to absorb the shock) — with the reasoning stated. |
| 4 | An explicit reason the distinction matters (durability: wealth persists across generations; income fluctuates). |

**Common errors:** conflating wealth and income; treating the budget crisis as affecting everyone
equally.

### A4 — mass incarceration / criminalization (12)

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | Alexander's definition of mass incarceration as a system of racial control, plus criminalization as the process of defining behaviours/populations as criminal. |
| 4 | The sitcom's sociological act identified: carrying the 1992 uprising (documented: *Honeymoon in L.A. (part 1)*, S6E120, 1992-09-24) into a comedy frame makes the state's treatment of Black citizens ordinary and unavoidable subject matter, not a special topic. |
| 4 | A claim about what that placement *does* (normalises, witnesses, or risks), stated as the student's interpretation. |

**Common errors:** relitigating 1992 as history; no account of what the *frame* (sitcom) does.

### A5 — boundaries of Blackness / category work (12)

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | "Boundaries of Blackness" defined as the contested edges of a real-but-not-fixed category, and "category work" as the labour of sorting, policing, and negotiating who fits. |
| 4 | An authority identified in the documented summary (Kojo's African/diaspora identity argument, Ep 7) with the *source* of that authority named (who is granted the right to rule on authenticity). |
| 4 | The stakes named concretely: what is won or lost by being ruled in or out. |

**Common errors:** answering "who is Black" as if it had a factual answer; no account of authority or
stakes.

## Part B — Applied essay (40)

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 8 | Documented facts stated and sourced (episode titles; air dates for originals), kept visibly separate from interpretation. |
| 12 | At least three concepts from the course, each doing *distinct* work on a specific moment. |
| 8 | An arguable, specific claim defended with evidence — not a summary. |
| 6 | Structure and agency (race as made vs. imposed) identified in the case. |
| 6 | An honest limit named: what the case cannot show, and what further source would settle it. |

**Common errors:** plot summary dressed as analysis; three concepts that are synonyms; an interpretation
passed off as documented fact; a limit that is really a hedge ("it might not be accurate") rather than
a specific gap.

**Marker's note:** for the comparison option (Q2), the "what changed / what did not" must be stated as
a claim about racial formation, not a list of differences. Apply the automatic rewrite once, ungraded,
when an interpretation is presented as fact.
