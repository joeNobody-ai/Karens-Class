# Homework 9 — Decode an image

**Due:** start of Module 10 · **Length:** 1 page · **Weight:** counts in the homework average (lowest two dropped)

## Task

1. Choose one image — a meme, an advertisement, a thumbnail, a headline, or a still from the screened
   scene — and run Stuart Hall's **encoding/decoding** on it: (a) what did the producer likely encode,
   and (b) give one **dominant** and one **oppositional** reading of the same image. Be specific to the
   image you chose.
2. In two or three sentences, use bell hooks's **oppositional gaze** to say who is allowed to look at
   whom in your image — and what changes when the object of the image looks back.

## Sources

- The reference site (the Cosby-origin and Debbie Allen chair cards): https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/
- Hall, "Encoding and Decoding in the Television Discourse" (1973) and hooks, *Black Looks* (1992),
  ch. 7 (from the module).
