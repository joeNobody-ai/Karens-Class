# Module 11 — Race and Health

**Big question.** Why does health follow the color line — even when access is equal?

**Where we start.** *Final Words* interrupts the usual finals panic with a more serious crisis — one
the published summary deliberately does not name — and that reframes what matters. The module uses that
quiet, reframing moment to ask a harder question than "who has insurance": why do health outcomes track
race even when the door to care is held open? *If I Should Die Before I Wake* (1991) asked the same
question about a life cut short on screen decades earlier.

## Sociological frame

1. **Health disparities**: systematic differences in health outcomes and access between groups, not
   explained by biology or individual choices alone.
2. **Structural racism**: racial inequality produced by the interlocking operation of institutions and
   histories, so that no single actor owns the outcome — here, as it writes itself into the body.
3. **Stigma** (Goffman, *Stigma*, 1963): a mark that discredits a person and changes how they are
   treated — including the stigma that keeps people from seeking care.
4. **Social determinants of health**: the conditions in which people are born, live, work, and age —
   housing, work, stress, exposure — that shape health more than the clinic does.

## Discussion questions

1. Why do health disparities persist even when access to care is equalised? Name the mechanism.
2. What is a social determinant the scene points to without naming it?
3. How does stigma operate as a *social* barrier to care, not just a feeling?
4. What does the crisis's interruption of "finals panic" argue about which worries are allowed to matter?
5. What data would you need to show that a disparity is structural rather than individual?

## Instructor notes

**If a student says "it's just lifestyle choices."** Push to the determinants: choices are made in
conditions people did not choose. Ask what the scene shows about the *conditions*, not the choices.

**If the unnamed crisis is a real-world event students recognise.** Keep the discussion on the
documented summary (what the published summary states) and the sociology, not on reconstructing details
the summary does not give.
