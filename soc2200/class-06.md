# Module 6 — Colourism and Intraracial Stratification

**Big question.** How does a hierarchy form inside a group that is itself stratified against?

**Where we start.** The second half of Ep 7 turns on Hazel's run for Miss Gilbert Hall — a pageant, a
beauty standard, and a ranking of bodies inside one community. Colourism is the reminder that
stratification never stops at the group boundary: it runs inside the group too. *Mr. Hillman* (1988)
shows the same machinery of status and respectability at work decades earlier.

## Sociological frame

1. **Colourism** (Hunter, *Race, Gender, and the Politics of Skin Tone*, 2005): a hierarchy that ranks
   people within a racial group by skin tone, usually privileging lighter tones.
2. **Skin tone**: the visible gradient of pigmentation that colourism turns into a status marker.
3. **Intraracial stratification**: inequality that operates *within* a racial group, layered on top of
   the inequality the group faces from outside.
4. **The politics of respectability** (Higginbotham, *Righteous Discontent*, 1993): the strategy of
   policing one's own group's presentation and behaviour to win acceptance from a hostile larger society.

## Discussion questions

1. Who sets the beauty standard in the pageant, and who is measured against it?
2. Is colourism a form of racism, or a distinct thing? Defend your answer.
3. What does the politics of respectability *promise* its practitioners — and what does it cost?
4. Why does a hierarchy inside a subordinated group often feel more painful than the one from outside?
5. What evidence would tell you whether the tone hierarchy is weakening or persisting?

## Instructor notes

**If a student says "we don't do that anymore."** Do not correct from authority; ask what *documented*
fact about the scene shows the standard being enforced, then let them test whether the mechanism is
period-specific or still running.

**If the discussion becomes about particular students' bodies.** Shut it down and redirect to the
scene: the course analyses standards, never the bodies in the room.
