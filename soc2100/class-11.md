# Module 11 — Work, the Economy, and the Working Poor

**Big question.** Why do people who work hard still struggle — and whose problem is that?

**Where we start.** We revisit Ep 9, this time on the money pressure: Rashida, struggling financially, looks for ways to make money while Hillman itself faces a budget crisis. The same episode shows a student scrambling and an institution short on funds — two faces of one economy. We ask what "work" is worth when it no longer pays for a life.

## Sociological frame

1. **Labor markets** (OpenStax 2021): the arenas where work is bought and sold — whose rules, not individual effort, set the price of labor.
2. **Precarity**: the condition of unstable, insecure work — no guaranteed hours, no benefits, no safety net — that has spread across the economy.
3. **The service economy**: the shift toward low-wage, often precarious service work, where "working hard" and "getting ahead" have come apart.
4. **The working poor**: people who work — often full-time — and remain poor, the single clearest rebuttal to the claim that hard work guarantees security.

## Discussion questions

1. If hard work guaranteed security, the working poor would not exist. What does their existence force us to conclude?
2. What is the difference between a low wage and a *precarious* job, and which is worse?
3. Why has "the service economy" made "working hard" and "getting ahead" come apart?
4. Is money pressure an individual problem or a structural one — and what evidence would settle it?
5. What would a labor-market account say about Rashida's scramble that a "she should budget better" account would not?

## Instructor notes

**If a student says "I worked my way through, so anyone can."** Honor the experience, then ask the structural question: what conditions made that path available, and what changes when those conditions do not hold?

**If the room blames individual spending.** Re-anchor to the data: the working-poor fact is about wages and hours, not budgeting. The homework makes them look at the actual numbers.
