# Module 5 — Health, Illness, and Stigma

**Big question.** Why does a health crisis become a moral problem — and who pays for the label?

**Where we start.** In *Final Words*, the panic of finals is interrupted by a more serious crisis that reframes what matters — a crisis the published summary deliberately does not name. We use that gap to study how illness gets *labelled*, and how stigma and moral judgment attach to conditions that are, in the first instance, biological.

## Sociological frame

1. **Health inequality** (OpenStax 2021): systematic differences in health and access to care that track social position — race, class, gender, and place.
2. **Stigma** (Goffman 1963): a mark that spoils identity — a condition made into a *discrediting* attribute through social response, not through the condition itself.
3. **Medicalization** (Conrad 2007): the process by which conditions once understood as moral, legal, or ordinary become defined as medical — a re-labeling with winners and losers.
4. **Social determinants of health**: the conditions of everyday life — housing, work, income, environment — that shape health outcomes more than individual behavior does.

## Discussion questions

1. Why does the same condition read as "shameful" in one context and "sympathetic" in another?
2. What does it mean that the published summary of *Final Words* does not name the crisis? What is the summary refusing to do?
3. Who pays for a stigma label — the person with the condition, or everyone around them?
4. When a condition is *medicalized*, what is gained and what is lost?
5. Does *If I Should Die Before I Wake* (1991-04-11) frame illness as behavior or circumstance? What is your evidence?

## Instructor notes

**If the room wants to name the unnamed crisis by guessing.** Decline to speculate on specifics; the point is the *gap itself*. Return to what the summary says and what it leaves out — that is the sociological datum.

**If a student uses a stigmatizing term for a condition.** Apply the vocabulary protocol: do not perform or define it; route to the numbered, cited terms and keep the discussion about the *label*, not the person.
