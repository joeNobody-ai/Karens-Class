# SOC 2100 — Social Problems · Midterm Examination — Answer Key

**Do not distribute to students.** Instructor material.

## Part A — Short answers (10 points each)

### 1. Claims-making

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | A correct definition of claims-making: the activity of asserting that a condition is a problem, naming a condition, attributing blame, and demanding action (Best). |
| 3 | A documented statement of both titles — *Irreconcilable Differences* (2026) and *Reconcilable Differences* (S1E1, 1987-09-24) — without inventing dialogue. |
| 3 | An application: the same family situation is *claimed* as unfixable in 2026 and fixable in 1987; the condition is constant, the claim changes. |

**Common errors:** treating the titles as evidence about the characters' real feelings rather than as claims; asserting a plot beat not in the published summary; forgetting that the air date is part of the documented fact.

### 2. Wealth versus income

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | Income = a flow (per year); wealth = a stock (assets minus debts), and the wealth gap exceeds the income gap. |
| 3 | A reason the wealth gap compounds (assets appreciate, are inherited, are unequally held to begin with). |
| 3 | A correct application: an endowment/budget crisis is a story about a *stock* (accumulated assets) being depleted or insufficient, not merely a single year's shortfall. |

**Common errors:** conflating wealth and income; asserting a specific dollar figure the student has not sourced; calling the budget crisis purely a flow problem without argument.

### 3. Racial formation

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | Definition: racial categories are created, contested, and changed through sociohistorical processes (Omi & Winant) — race is made, not found. |
| 3 | A documented anchor: the diaspora argument in Ep 7 (first half) as given in the published summary. |
| 3 | Application: the argument is *itself* an act of formation — drawing and contesting a boundary over who is inside a category. |

**Common errors:** reducing racial formation to "racism"; inventing specific lines of the argument; treating the category in dispute as fixed rather than contested.

### 4. Doing gender

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | Definition: gender as an ongoing accomplishment, produced in interaction and held accountable to norms (West & Zimmerman). |
| 3 | A documented anchor: the Miss Gilbert Hall contest (Ep 7, second half) and *Mr. Hillman* (S1E17, 1988-02-25), both named with the original's air date. |
| 3 | Application: the contest is a site where gender is *judged against a standard*, and the men's version makes the normally-invisible standard visible. |

**Common errors:** describing gender as a fixed trait; moralizing about the contestants instead of analyzing the standard; omitting the air date of *Mr. Hillman*.

### 5. Stigma

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | Definition: a mark that spoils identity, created by social response rather than the condition itself (Goffman). |
| 3 | A correct, documented statement of what the *Final Words* summary does and does not say — it names a "more serious crisis" without naming it. |
| 3 | Interpretation (labeled as such): the refusal to name is itself significant — the summary declines to attach the label, which is a move about labeling. |

**Common errors:** guessing what the unnamed crisis is and asserting it as fact; missing that the *gap* in the summary is the sociological datum.

### 6. Credentialing

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | Definition: degrees function as sorting devices — tickets to positions — more than as measures of learning (Collins). |
| 3 | A documented anchor: *Take This Job and Love It* (S2E41, 1989-03-16) with its air date. |
| 3 | Application: for a working student a degree is a *cost* (time, money, forgone income) purchased against a *future position*; the tradeoff is credentialing, not just study. |

**Common errors:** treating the degree as pure learning; omitting the cost side of the tradeoff; no air date for the original.

## Part B — Applied question (40 points)

| Points | A full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 8 | Working definitions of *structure* (durable arrangements) and *agency* (people's choices), and a stated position on which better explains the scenes. |
| 8 | At least one documented fact per text (episode title; original title and air date; a published-summary detail), kept separate from interpretation. |
| 8 | Interpretations clearly marked as the student's reading, with no fabricated dialogue or statistics. |
| 8 | The strongest version of the *other* side engaged, not caricatured. |
| 8 | An honest limit: what the chosen account cannot explain, named concretely. |

**Common errors:** asserting an interpretation as documented fact; arguing both sides equally without taking a position; inventing a plot beat to make the argument land; a "limit" that is really a hedge ("it might not be accurate").

**Marker's note:** award full credit for an *honest, well-supported* argument, not for a particular conclusion. The two things that cap an otherwise strong essay are fabricated episode facts and an unmarked slide from fact to reading.
