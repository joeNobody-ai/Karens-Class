# Module 15 — Capstone: Naming a Problem, Proposing a Response

**Big question.** Can you name a problem precisely, and defend a response to it?

**Where we start.** The season ends at the gala, where the crisis, the relationships, and the family situation all reach breaking points and major decisions are made about the college's future. We close the course by doing what we have practiced all term: naming a problem precisely, separating fact from interpretation, and defending a response with its costs and beneficiaries.

## Sociological frame

1. **Synthesis**: holding several concepts from the term at once against a single case, so that no one lens does all the work.
2. **Public sociology** (Burawoy 2004): sociology addressed to a public, aimed at usefulness — making a claim a non-specialist could act on.
3. **The response**: a proposed intervention, stated with its *costs* and *beneficiaries* — the discipline that keeps a proposal honest.

## Discussion questions

1. What does a *precise* name for a problem buy you that a vague one does not?
2. Every response has costs. Who pays them in the season's final decision?
3. What is the difference between proposing a response and *defending* one?
4. *A Rock, a River, a Lena* (1993-07-02) and *A Gift & The Curse* both end in a choice about a future. What makes a choice *public*?
5. Which concept from this term would you still be using in five years — and for what?

## Instructor notes

**If a student's capstone is a slogan rather than a claim.** Ask them to name the claimant, the condition, and what the account leaves out — the three-part test for a precise name.

**If a student proposes a response with no costs.** Send them back to the beneficiaries: a response that costs nothing and benefits everyone is usually a response that has not been examined yet.
