# Module 2 — Poverty and Economic Inequality

**Big question.** Is poverty a failure of people, or a feature of the structure — and how would you know?

**Where we start.** Hillman's budget crisis erupts and the students organize — a campus with an endowment problem standing in for a society with an inequality problem. We use the crisis to separate three things that everyday talk collapses: poverty (a condition), inequality (a distribution), and wealth versus income (two different measures of both).

## Sociological frame

1. **Poverty** (Best 2021): the condition of lacking the resources a society treats as a minimum; *officially* defined in many places by a threshold, which is itself a claim worth examining.
2. **Inequality**: the *distribution* of resources across a population — a relational fact, distinct from how much any one person has.
3. **Wealth versus income** (OpenStax 2021): income is a flow (what comes in each year); wealth is a stock (what is owned minus what is owed) — and wealth is far more unequally distributed than income.
4. **The working poor**: people who are employed, often full-time, yet still fall below the poverty line — the sharpest evidence against a simple "individual failure" account.

## Discussion questions

1. Is the budget crisis at Hillman a "poverty" problem or an "inequality" problem — and what turns on the difference?
2. Why does the wealth gap grow even when the income gap is stable? What does that tell you about which measure matters?
3. The working poor are employed and still poor. Which account — individual or structural — does that fact put under the most pressure?
4. What is the poverty threshold *claiming*, and who gets to set it?
5. What would you need to know before you could say the budget crisis "caused" any one student's hardship?

## Instructor notes

**If a student insists "I know poor people who…".** Redirect to the data question: a case is not a rate. Ask what the structural account would predict and what the anecdote can and cannot show.

**If the wealth/income distinction blurs.** Slow to the single image: income is a paycheck; wealth is a house minus a mortgage. Every other contrast follows from that pair.
