# Module 3 — Racial Inequality

**Big question.** How does racial inequality persist even when nobody in the room is a racist?

**Where we start.** In the end-of-year talent show, Kojo is caught up in arguments over African and diaspora identity — a dispute about *categories* that becomes a dispute about *who belongs to which*. We use the first half of Ep 7 to study racial inequality as something produced through classification, not only through individual animus.

## Sociological frame

1. **Racial inequality** (Omi & Winant 1986): durable differences in resources and life chances that track racial categories — a *result*, not a belief.
2. **Racial formation** (Omi & Winant 1986): the sociohistorical process by which racial categories are created, contested, and changed — race is *made*, not found.
3. **Colourism**: unequal treatment of people based on skin tone within and across racialized groups — a hierarchy that runs *inside* categories, not just between them.
4. **Structural racism** (OpenStax 2021): racial inequality produced by the ordinary operation of institutions — policies, norms, and distributions — even absent individual racist intent.

## Discussion questions

1. If nobody in the room is a racist, what is still *doing* the inequality? Name a mechanism, not a motive.
2. What does the diaspora argument show about who gets to define a category — the people inside it or the people outside it?
3. Is colourism a form of racial inequality, or a separate axis? What evidence would settle it?
4. The 1990 throwback *A World Alike* poses the same "who is like us" question. What changed in how the two eras frame it?
5. What would a constructionist notice about the very phrase "racial inequality"?

## Instructor notes

**If the room conflates "structural racism" with "everyone is a secret racist."** Re-state the definition: the claim is about outcomes produced by ordinary institutional operation, not about private motives. Keep motive and mechanism apart.

**If the discussion turns to who "really" belongs to a diaspora.** Step back to the category level: the sociological question is *how the boundary is drawn and by whom*, not *where the true boundary is*. The protocol applies — no student is asked to adjudicate their own group.
