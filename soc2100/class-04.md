# Module 4 — Gender Inequality

**Big question.** What does a pageant reveal about how gender inequality is produced and judged?

**Where we start.** Hazel runs for Miss Gilbert Hall in the second half of Ep 7 — a pageant that is at once a competition and a machine for judging women. We use it to ask how gender is *done* rather than simply *had*, and how a "women's" contest becomes a site where inequality is produced and measured.

## Sociological frame

1. **Gender inequality** (OpenStax 2021): systematic differences in resources, power, and status that track gender — a distribution, not a set of opinions.
2. **Doing gender** (West & Zimmerman 1987): gender as an ongoing *accomplishment* — something people produce in interaction, held accountable to norms, not a fixed trait they simply have.
3. **The second shift** (Hochschild 1989): the unpaid domestic and care labor that falls disproportionately to women *after* the paid workday ends.
4. **The gender pay gap**: the difference between what women and men earn, measured against the same work and hours — a persistent, well-documented inequality that resists "individual choice" explanations.

## Discussion questions

1. A pageant *appears* to reward individuals. What is it actually judging, and who sets the standard?
2. What does "doing gender" capture that "being a woman" does not?
3. Why does *Mr. Hillman* (1988-02-25) make the machinery visible in a way the Miss Gilbert Hall contest does not?
4. Is the second shift a "gender" problem or a "work" problem? What turns on the answer?
5. If the pay gap persists after controlling for hours and occupation, what is left doing the work?

## Instructor notes

**If a student argues "women just choose lower-paying fields."** Do not dismiss it; ask what *shapes* those choices (who is steered where, who does the care work) and whether "choice" fully explains the controlled gap.

**If the pageant talk slides into mockery of the contestants.** Pull it back to the machinery: the sociological target is the standard and who sets it, not the people judged by it.
