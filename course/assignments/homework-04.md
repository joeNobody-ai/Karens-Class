# Homework 4 — Vocabulary as evidence

**Due:** start of Session 5 · **Length:** 1.5 pages · **Weight:** counts in the homework average

## Task

Language is data. Collect the words the two shows use for the same activities and analyse what the
vocabulary assumes.

1. **Build a table** with two columns: *1987–1993 terms* and *2026 terms*, for the general area of
   romantic and sexual relationships. Aim for at least six terms per column. Use the episode summaries
   and callback cards for the revival, and the original episode list for the earlier era. Where a term
   is your own gloss rather than a word in the text, mark it.
2. **For each pair** (or nearest equivalent), state what the word assumes about: duration, exclusivity,
   formality, and who is expected to initiate.
3. **One paragraph.** What does "cuffing season" imply that "going together" does not — and what does
   that implication tell you about the conditions under which people form relationships now?
4. **One counter-example.** Find a term in either era that does not fit your pattern, and say what it
   does to your analysis. A pattern that survives its counter-example is worth having.

## Criteria (out of 10)

| Points | For |
|---|---|
| 4 | At least 12 terms, correctly attributed to an era, with glosses marked as yours where they are |
| 3 | The four assumptions are applied consistently to each pair |
| 2 | The paragraph makes an argument about conditions, not just about slang |
| 1 | The counter-example is real and honestly handled |

**Trap:** do not assume the older vocabulary was more moral and the newer one more casual. The evidence
is the words, and what the words make possible.
