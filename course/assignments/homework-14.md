# Homework 14 — Figure, source, denominator

**Due:** start of Session 15 · **Length:** 2 pages · **Weight:** counts in the homework average

## Task

The state, the street, and the ledger, in numbers you found yourself.

1. **Wealth and income, side by side.** Find two current, citable figures from primary sources (federal
   statistical agencies, the Federal Reserve's Survey of Consumer Finances, peer-reviewed studies):
   - median household **wealth** by race, and
   - median household **income** by race,
   for the most recent year available. Report each with source, table or report name, URL, retrieval
   date, and the comparison being made.
2. **The mechanism.** In 400 words, explain how income gaps and wealth gaps can move in opposite
   directions. Name mechanisms: inheritance, home equity and its histories, credit access, pensions,
   student and consumer debt, business capital, incarceration's economic effects. Cite at least two
   sources.
3. **The 1992 text.** Original S6E120 (*Honeymoon in L.A. (part 1)*, 1992-09-24) opens a season with a
   police-brutality trial and the uprising that followed. In 300 words: describe what the episode does
   with that material, and what it assumes about its audience. Facts only — use the episode's documented
   summary and, if you want context, cite one historical source on the 1992 Los Angeles uprising.
4. **Where is that tradition now?** In 2026 Episode 9, students organise against an institutional
   crisis. In 250 words, compare what each text asks its audience to do with what they have seen.
5. **One sentence.** State the strongest argument that the revival's organising plot is a different kind
   of political storytelling than the 1992 premiere — and then say whether you believe it.
