# Homework 13 — Steel-man and falsify

**Due:** start of Session 14 · **Length:** 2 pages · **Weight:** counts in the homework average

## Task

This is the discipline's hardest intellectual move: state your opponent's case better than they did, then
decide what evidence would settle the matter.

1. **Choose one family or intimacy plot** from either era — for example, a character's relationship with
   a parent, a couple's conflict over money, or the show's treatment of a single parent. Describe it in
   150 words.
2. **Steel-man the culture explanation.** In 300 words, argue that the behaviour in this plot is
   explained by *culture* — transmitted values, norms, expectations. Make it as strong as you can. Cite
   at least one real source that makes a version of this argument.
3. **Steel-man the structural explanation.** In 300 words, argue that it is explained by *structure* —
   money, jobs, housing, schooling, policing, credit. Cite at least one real source.
4. **Falsification.** For each explanation, state what evidence would show it is wrong. Be specific:
   what would you need to observe, measure, or read?
5. **Verdict.** In 150 words, say which explanation the text supports, and — this is the graded part —
   say whether the text *could* settle the question at all. A television drama about six people cannot
   demonstrate a population-level mechanism. What can it do instead?

## Criteria (out of 10)

| Points | For |
|---|---|
| 4 | Both explanations are steel-manned and sourced — no straw men |
| 3 | Falsification conditions are concrete and obtainable |
| 2 | The verdict distinguishes what a text can and cannot demonstrate |
| 1 | Sources are cited accurately (author, year, argument) |

**The trap:** most students steel-man the explanation they already agree with. If your culture paragraph
is weaker than your structure paragraph, you have not done the assignment — you have restated your view
twice.
