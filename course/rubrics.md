# Rubrics

Short, reusable, and used every week. If a rubric takes longer to read than the assignment took to grade,
it is the wrong rubric.

---

## 1. Weekly homework (out of 10)

| Points | Criterion | The question you are asking |
|---|---|---|
| 3–4 | **Evidence discipline** | Are documented facts, interpretations and hypotheses kept apart? Are sources cited with enough detail to be checked? |
| 2–3 | **Analytical work** | Does the submission make a claim, or does it summarise? Is the claim connected to a course concept? |
| 2 | **Task fidelity** | Did they do *this* assignment — the specific analytical move it asked for? |
| 1 | **Precision of prose** | Clear, no padding, no throat-clearing. |

**Written feedback formula:** one thing that worked, one thing to fix, one sentence on evidence handling.
Three sentences, every time. Students read these; they do not read numbers.

**The automatic rewrite request:** a submission that asserts a population claim from a sample of one, or
that presents an interpretation as documented fact. Return it once, ungraded, with the rewrite condition
stated. Almost everyone fixes it, and nobody has to lose marks to learn it.

---

## 2. Participation and discussion leadership (15% of the course grade)

Assessed across the term, with one session formally led by each student.

| Points | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 4 | **Facilitation (session led)** — opened the material, brought at least two members in, kept the room on the text, managed time, closed with a question rather than a summary |
| 3 | **Analytical contribution** — offered claims with evidence; built on others rather than replacing them |
| 3 | **Norms** — respected the no-spokesperson rule; engaged with the strongest version of a view they disagreed with; allowed silence |
| 2 | **Text discipline** — kept the room anchored; corrected or checked claims rather than letting them pass |
| 3 | **Intellectual honesty** — changed position when the evidence required it, said so, and named what changed their mind |

**What does not earn participation credit:** airtime, repetition, performing certainty, or the phrase
"I just feel like".

---

## 3. Data exercise (homeworks 5, 10, 14)

| Points | Criterion | Automatic failures |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | **Source quality** — primary source, current, correctly described | Citing a news article *about* a study; citing an advocacy summary as primary |
| 3 | **Completeness of reporting** — figure, source, year, URL, retrieval date, denominator, comparison group | Missing denominator or comparison group |
| 2 | **Honesty about limits** — "what this cannot tell us" is genuinely about measurement, not a hedge | "It might not be accurate" (not a limit; a shrug) |
| 2 | **Use** — the figure is used in an argument without overclaiming | Treating a correlational figure as causal |

---

## 4. Capstone memo (homework 15, 10% of the course grade)

Out of 20, scaled to course weight.

| Points | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 4 | **Claim** — arguable, specific, unmistakably the student's |
| 5 | **Evidence** — the paired moments do genuinely comparative work |
| 4 | **Concept use** — three or more concepts used, not named |
| 3 | **Honesty section** — tiers handled explicitly; one limitation of the shows as evidence named |
| 2 | **Stakes** — consequences argued |
| 2 | **Prose and citation** — readable by a non-specialist; consistent style; retrieval dates |

**Presentation (required, ungraded):** claim, best evidence, unsettled question, in 90 seconds.

---

## 5. Self-assessment prompt (Session 15, ungraded — for the professor's own use)

Hand this out and collect it before the final. It is the best course evaluation you will get, and it takes
five minutes of class time.

1. Which session changed your mind about something, and what was the evidence?
2. Name one concept you will still use in five years.
3. Where did this course ask you to be dishonest with evidence, or let you get away with it?
4. What is the strongest argument that this course should not exist?
5. One thing you would teach differently if you were teaching it.
