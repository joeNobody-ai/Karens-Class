# Final examination

**SOC 4150 · *A Different World*: Black Experience and American Society**
**Session:** 15 (or your institution's examination period) · **Weight:** 30% of the course grade
**Total:** 50 points

**Two permitted formats — choose one and announce it at the start of term:**

| Format | Time | Materials |
|---|---|---|
| **In-class** (recommended) | 2 hours | Exhibits provided; no notes, no devices. Keeps the applied question honest |
| **Take-home** | 4 hours, single sitting | Course readings and reference site permitted; citations required |

Either way the paper is comprehensive, weighted toward Sessions 9–15, and structured around **transfer**:
can you do this work on material the course did not cover?

---

## Part A — Short answers (3 × 5 = 15 points)

Answer **all three**. 200–250 words each.

**A1.** A student claims: *"Colourism inside Black institutions exists because Black people have
internalised racism."* Assess that claim. Name one mechanism it identifies, one it misses, and one piece
of evidence that would move the argument forward.

**A2.** Whitley's father cuts up her credit cards in S4E73 (*Whitley's Last Supper*, 1990-10-11). State
what is *documented* about that plot, then give one structural reading and one culture-based reading of
it, and say what evidence would distinguish between them.

**A3.** Define "racialized organizations" and use it to explain one feature of Hillman's budget crisis in
2026 Episode 9 — in a way that does not require anyone in the episode to be acting out of racial
animus.

## Part B — Applied question: two documents the course did not assign (15 points)

Below are **documented summaries** of two episodes of the original series that were not assigned in this
course. Read them as documents, the way you have read everything else this term.

> **Exhibit A — *If You Like Pilgrim Coladas* (original, S2E28, first aired 1988-11-24).**
> Whitley and Kim stay at the dorm for Thanksgiving. Depressed by the prospect of Thanksgiving dinner at
> the Pit, they decide to go out. Kim uses a fake ID to get them into a club. Whitley does not realise the
> waiter has served them non-alcoholic drinks; she ends up performing a number and dancing on the bar. Kim
> spots Col. Taylor in the crowd and tries to warn her; Col. Taylor helps Whitley fight off an overzealous
> club patron.

> **Exhibit B — *Interior Desecration* (original, S6E122, first aired 1992-10-08).**
> Ron mediates Dwayne and Whitley's conflict over whose belongings will go and whose will stay in their
> new apartment — and gets his own room downstairs as a result. The underclassmen engage in a stepping
> challenge, hoping to promote unity.

**Your task (≈600 words):**

1. **One claim, one observation.** For each exhibit, state one thing that is *documented* and one thing
   that is your *interpretation*. Keep them visibly separate.
2. **Three concepts.** Analyse the pair using **three** course concepts (for example: negotiated order ·
   dramaturgy · ritual and solidarity · kin work · respectability · intragenerational class · negotiated
   space · racialized organizations).
3. **The two-institution reading.** Both exhibits concern a *space* — the dorm and the club; the shared
   apartment. What does each text treat space as: shelter, status, money, or a relationship being
   negotiated? Support your answer from the summaries.
4. **The generational claim.** Exhibit B pairs an older couple dividing belongings with younger students
   seeking unity. Using Sessions 3, 6 and 11, state what this pairing suggests the franchise thinks is
   *transmitted* across generations — and say what you cannot establish from the summary alone.
5. **Transfer.** Connect the pair to **one** callback card of your choosing from the reference site, and
   explain what the connection shows that neither the exhibits nor the card shows alone. Include the card's
   URL and its tier.

## Part C — Essay (20 points)

Answer **one**. 1,100–1,300 words. At least **four** sources, at least two of them from the course reading,
and at least one primary source (data, an archival item, or documented episode facts).

**C1. The constitutive claim.** The course's central claim is that Black experience is not a topic
adjacent to American society but one of the terrains on which it is organised. Defend, qualify, or reject
that claim using the franchise as your case. Deal directly with the strongest objection: that a
television comedy cannot bear this weight because it is written to sell advertising and streaming
subscriptions. What can a text that is also a commodity show a sociologist — and what can it never show?

**C2. The long 1992.** Original S6E120 (*Honeymoon in L.A. (part 1)*, 1992-09-24) opened a season with a
police-brutality trial and the uprising that followed. 2026 Episode 9 has students organising against a
budget crisis. Write an essay on what changed in the 34 years between those two texts *in what television
is willing to put at the centre of a Black ensemble drama* — and be explicit about the evidence you are
using, since industrial and editorial decisions leave different traces than social conditions do.

**C3. Teaching this course.** You have been asked to redesign a standard introduction-to-sociology
syllabus around this case. Write an essay justifying (or refusing) the redesign: what would be gained in
concept coverage, what would be lost, and how you would handle the fact that the case is a commercial
entertainment product rather than a data source. Cite the course's own concepts and at least one source
that critiques the use of popular culture in teaching.

- Every empirical claim: **source and year**. Anything online: retrieval date.
- Evidence tiers must be visible throughout (documented / interpretation / hypothesis).
- AI assistance: structure and language only, with disclosure; the analysis must be yours and defensible
  orally. Undisclosed AI-generated analysis is a violation.
- Exhibits in Part B may be quoted; cite them as Exhibit A / Exhibit B.
