# Midterm key and grading rubric — INSTRUCTOR MATERIAL

**Do not distribute to students.** Model answers here are *standards*, not scripts: a strong student will
take a different route and should be rewarded for it. What follows is what a full-credit answer must
contain.

---

## Part A1 (5 points) — The two openings

| Points | What the answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 2 | **Documented:** the two premiere dates (24 September 1987; 24 September 2026 — 39 years to the day) and the two titles (*Reconcilable Differences*; *Irreconcilable Differences*). Nothing more. Any claim that this pairing was *intended* as a statement is not documented. |
| 2 | **Interpretation:** a defensible reading that the one-letter change reframes the original's reconciliation as a contemporary conflict, and that launching on the same calendar date stages the revival as an inheritance. Must be labelled as a reading. |
| 1 | **Hypothesis / unsettleable:** whether the writers chose the date and title as a deliberate thesis. Settling it would need a writer's statement, a production document, or an interview. Students should note that absent such evidence the question stays open — and that saying so is not a failure of nerve. |

**Common errors to mark down:** presenting the 39-year gap as *proof* of intent; or, conversely, refusing
to say anything interpretive at all ("we cannot know, so there is nothing to say").

---

## Part A2 (5 points) — Cost allocation

| Points | What the answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 2 | **1989:** the cost falls on the radio host (suspended from the station) while the protesters are not punished; the dean protects the institution's position without disciplining dissent. Cost type: standing/access, plus the institutional lesson about which speech is protected. |
| 2 | **2026:** the cost falls on the student who spoke (public criticism, social and institutional exposure). Cost type: standing, reputation, and the risk attached to speech. Ideally notes that the mechanism by which the cost is imposed is less visible in the 2026 episode. |
| 1 | **Mechanism judgment:** a defensible conclusion either way, but it must be argued with evidence. Most answers will conclude the 1989 episode is more specific: it names the decision-maker, the sanction and the exemption, whereas the 2026 text leaves the enforcement mechanism partly off screen. |

**Reward** answers that notice the interesting asymmetry: in 1989 the sanction is *formal and narrow*
(one host, one station); in 2026 it is *informal and diffuse* (a public call-out, no stated rule). That
observation is worth full marks even if the student draws the opposite conclusion about specificity.

## Part B (10 points) — Essay

| Points | Criterion | Notes for the marker |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | **Concept use** | At least three course concepts used correctly (ritual, effervescence, boundary work, stigma, structural violence, racial formation, cost allocation, respectability, etc.). Naming without using earns partial credit at best. |
| 3 | **Comparative analysis** | The essay must show something the comparison produces that a single text cannot. Answers that analyse each text in turn and never bring them into relation cap at 2 of 3. |
| 2 | **Evidence** | Episode facts accurate (titles, dates, season/episode). Claims about what happens on screen either documented or flagged as the student's own observation. |
| 1 | **Source use** | At least two reading citations, used for something, not decorative. |
| 1 | **Precision of prose** | Clear, no padding, argumentative movement. |

### B1 — Ritual and its price: what a full-credit answer contains

- Durkheim's concept used properly: shared practice generating a collective force, not "a tradition".
- At least one **boundary**: in 1988, who is on the step line and who is removed (Whitley's insult about
  "the rhythmless Freddie" gets her thrown off the squad); in 2026, who is included as alumni and who is
  peripheral.
- At least one **labour** item: the squad's rehearsal, the band, the alumni office, the (mostly women's)
  care work that organises the event.
- At least one thing **concealed**: in the revival, the failing marriage behind the celebration; in the
  original, the status competition dressed as school spirit.
- A comparison claim: e.g. the 2026 text assumes ritual literacy and therefore shows less of its
  machinery — which means the audience sees belonging but not the labour that produces it.

### B2 — The heaviest slot: what a full-credit answer contains

- Correct handling of the 1991 episode's documented facts: a speech-class assignment leads Josie to
  disclose she is HIV-positive; the class re-examines its beliefs; guests included Tisha Campbell and
  Whoopi Goldberg.
- **Honesty requirement:** the revival episode's crisis is *not named in the published summary*. An answer
  that asserts what it is has broken the evidence rule and caps at 4. An answer that says "I have not been
  able to document what the crisis is, so I analyse its structural position in the season instead" is
  demonstrating exactly the skill being taught.
- Goffman (stigma, disclosure, spoiled identity) and/or Cohen (the boundaries of Black politics and
  which crises get recognised) used analytically.
- The 1991/2026 contrast: a network special episode addressed to a mass audience in a period of official
  silence, versus a streaming scene inside a season built for continuity. Students may argue either way
  about which can "do more"; they must argue.

### B3 — The category under pressure: what a full-credit answer contains

- Both texts read as *category work*: what "Black" is being asked to mean, and by whom.
- The 1990 dilemma stated accurately: an exchange student in Hillman's anti-apartheid group; a scholarship
  funded by a company with South African investments; a boycott proposal that would cost a Black student
  her funding; the show's resolution in favour of the friend.
- Omi and Winant used to frame the argument as racial formation rather than as confusion or
  misunderstanding.
- A specific answer to "who is written out": students should identify the party whose interests are
  marginalised by the tight definition in each text — not simply say "everyone is included".

**Marker's note:** the strongest essays in this course are almost always the ones that take a position
*and* state the strongest case against it. Watch for that, and reward it explicitly.

---

## Return-of-marks protocol

Return the midterm with one paragraph of feedback per student: one thing the answer did well, one thing
the evidence handling needs, and (if relevant) whether the student's tier discipline is improving. Do
not write grades on the front page and hand the pile back; the session-10 slot is worth the ten minutes
it takes to say something specific.
