## Instructions

Answer **Part A** and **Part B**. Submit as one document. Cite your sources; for anything online, include
the retrieval date. You may use the course readings, the reference site, and your own notes.

**The evidence rule applies.** In every answer, keep these apart: **documented** facts (episode titles,

## Part A — Short answers (2 × 5 points = 10 points)

Answer **both**. 250 words each. A precise answer with a source beats a long one without.

### A1. The two openings

The 2026 revival premiered on 24 September 2026; the original premiered on 24 September 1987. The
original's first episode is titled *Reconcilable Differences*; the revival's is *Irreconcilable
Differences*. Using Session 1's framework (documented / interpretation / hypothesis), state what can be
*documented* about this pairing, what is a defensible *interpretation*, and what cannot be settled with
the evidence available. Then say why the distinction matters for how a sociologist writes about a text.

### A2. Cost allocation

In original S2E32 (*Radio Free Hillman*, 1989-01-05) students hold a sit-in after their campus radio show
is pulled; the dean declines to punish the protesters and suspends the student radio host instead. In 2026
Episode 2, a student is publicly called out after expressing an opinion. Identify, for each text, **who
bears the cost** of the conflict and **what kind of cost** it is (money, standing, time, opportunity,
safety). Then state which of the two texts is more specific about the mechanism that allocates the cost,
and give your evidence.

## Part B — Essay (10 points)

Answer **one** of the following. 900–1,000 words. Use at least three course concepts and at least two
sources from the reading.

### B1. Ritual and its price

Using original S2E29 (*A Stepping Stone*, 1988-12-01) and 2026 Episode 3 (*Center de Stage*), argue
whether homecoming functions as ritual in Durkheim's sense. Your answer must identify at least one
boundary the ritual draws, at least one kind of labour it depends on, and one thing it conceals. Then
state what the comparison between the two eras shows that neither episode alone could.

### B2. The heaviest slot

Original S4E92 (*If I Should Die Before I Wake*, 1991-04-11) put an HIV disclosure at the centre of a
prime-time sitcom. 2026 Episode 5 puts an unnamed serious crisis inside finals week. Compare the two
episodes' strategies for handling a health crisis: what does each *make sayable*, and what does each
leave outside the frame? Use Goffman on stigma and/or Cohen on the boundaries of Black politics, and
name one thing that television could do in 1991 that it cannot do in 2026 — or argue that it can.

### B3. The category under pressure

Using the diaspora argument in 2026 Episode 7 and original S3E60 (*A World Alike*, 1990-02-15), argue
whether "Black" is doing descriptive work, political work, or both, in each text. Identify who is written
out when the category is defined tightly, and what the 1990 episode's dilemma (a boycott that would cost
a student her scholarship) reveals about the politics of principle. Use Omi and Winant on racial
formation.
