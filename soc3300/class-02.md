# Module 2 — Ritual and Collective Effervescence

**Big question.** What does a group feel when it does something together — and why does it matter?

**Where we start.** Module 1 asked what culture *is*; this module asks what it *does to a group*. We
return to the homecoming (the 2026 *Center de Stage* and its 1988 throwback, *A Stepping Stone*,
1988-12-01) and read it through Émile Durkheim's account of ritual: the claim that when people assemble
around a shared symbol and do the same thing at the same time, they generate a heightened, self-reinforcing
collective feeling — *effervescence* — out of which the group's sense of itself is made. The published
summary of *Center de Stage* gives us the assembly (a headlining performance, the return of Dwayne and
Whitley) and the original gives us the ritual form (the step).

## Sociological frame

1. **Durkheim** (Émile Durkheim, *The Elementary Forms of the Religious Life*, 1912): society is held
   together not only by interests but by shared representations, and these are generated in assembly.
2. **Ritual**: a standardized, repeated, symbol-laden sequence of action that a group performs together —
   the step, the homecoming assembly — whose meaning lies in the doing, not in any message stated.
3. **Collective effervescence**: the heightened emotional state a group reaches when it acts in unison,
   which Durkheim argued is the crucible in which sacred symbols and group identity are forged.
4. **Solidarity**: the felt bond of membership the ritual produces — mechanical (sameness) or organic
   (interdependence) — which outlasts the ritual moment and can be called back later.

## Discussion questions

1. Durkheim argued that effervescence is *generated* by assembly, not merely released by it. What evidence from the two scenes would support or challenge that?
2. Is a step routine a ritual in Durkheim's sense, or just a coordinated performance? What is the difference that matters?
3. The published summary states the marriage is strained. Can a group feel effervescent while its central figures are in conflict? What does your answer imply about solidarity?
4. The "aunties" are documented as original-cast arrivals. Why does *the return of past participants* intensify collective feeling — and what theory from Module 1 helps explain it?
5. If ritual makes solidarity, what happens to a group that stops assembling? Can the solidarity be stored, or does it decay?

## Instructor notes

**If the room treats effervescence as mere "hype."** Re-anchor: Durkheim's claim is that the feeling
*produces* the sacred and the group's self-image — it is causal, not decorative. Ask what would be lost
if the step were performed by one person alone.

**If a student claims to know what the cast "really felt."** That is neither documented nor a reading;
it is projection. Redirect to what the text and the summary *show* — the assembly, the return, the
tensions — and ask what a Durkheimian would predict from those facts.
