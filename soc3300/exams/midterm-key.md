# SOC 3300 — Midterm Examination — Answer Key (INSTRUCTOR MATERIAL)

**Do not distribute to students.** This key states what a full-credit answer must contain and the
marker's notes. It does not script a single "correct" answer; credit is for the *structure* of the
analysis, not for matching wording.

## Part A — 12 points each (60 total)

### A1 — Geertz vs. Swidler (12)

| Points | Full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | Accurate one-sentence definitions: Geertz — culture as a web of meaning people inhabit; Swidler — culture as a toolkit of strategies/resources people deploy |
| 4 | Both theories applied to the *same* documented fact (headliner + marriage tensions), each revealing something distinct |
| 4 | Explicit contrast: what Geertz's lens makes visible (shared meaning, the homecoming's symbolism) vs. what Swidler's makes visible (the step/headliner as a resource deployed under constraint) |

**Common errors:** defining the two as synonyms ("both say culture matters"); applying each to a
*different* fact so no real contrast is made; treating "meaning" as Swidler's term.

### A2 — Collective effervescence (12)

| Points | Full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | Correct definition *and* the causal sequence stated (assembly → heightened shared feeling → solidarity that outlasts the moment) |
| 4 | Testable indicators named for a step routine: synchronization, call-and-response, shared symbol, audience-as-participant |
| 4 | At least one named condition that would count *against* the claim (e.g., perfunctory execution, absent shared symbol) |

**Common errors:** reducing effervescence to "hype" or "energy" with no causal claim; offering only
*confirming* evidence and no disconfirming condition; asserting what participants "really felt."

### A3 — Gatekeeping vs. culture industry (12)

| Points | Full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | Gatekeeping defined in the production-of-culture sense (positions that select what gets made / who gets access), and a *contrast* with Adorno/Horkheimer (standardization, commodity, pacification) |
| 4 | Both documented facts used (Cosby spin-off origin; Debbie Allen's chairing) |
| 4 | A *defended* verdict on which theory fits better, with a reason tied to the facts |

**Common errors:** defining gatekeeping but never actually contrasting the two theories; treating the two
facts as "good" or "bad" rather than as evidence about *who controls production*.

### A4 — Three decoding positions (12)

| Points | Full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | The three positions stated and the negotiated/oppositional distinction made precisely (negotiated accepts the frame in part; oppositional rejects the frame) |
| 4 | A coherent negotiated reading of the call-out (accepts *that* speech is answerable but contests the terms) |
| 4 | A coherent oppositional reading (re-codes the "call-out" against its grain), and the difference shown |

**Common errors:** using "negotiated" and "oppositional" interchangeably; inventing dialogue or motive to
support a reading; a negotiated reading that is really just "mixed feelings."

### A5 — Gray and the double burden (12)

| Points | Full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 4 | The "argument, not window" distinction explained (TV struggles *over* blackness rather than reflecting it) |
| 4 | The double burden named (entertain *and* represent a community to itself and others) and attributed correctly to Gray / Means Coleman |
| 4 | One documented production fact applied, showing what the argument *is* in this case |

**Common errors:** paraphrasing Gray as "representation matters"; attributing the audience-division point
to Gray instead of Means Coleman; no actual application to a fact.

## Part B — Applied essay (40)

| Points | Full-credit answer must contain |
|---|---|
| 8 | A plain, arguable thesis stated in 1–2 sentences at the top |
| 10 | Two concepts, each doing *distinct* work on the evidence (not synonyms) |
| 8 | At least two documented facts (named episodes and air dates) carrying the argument |
| 6 | Documented facts and interpretation visibly separated throughout |
| 5 | One honest limit named — what the evidence cannot show |
| 3 | Prose: tight, no padding, an argument not a summary |

**Common errors:** an essay that *summarizes* modules instead of arguing; concepts stacked but each
saying the same thing; a "limit" that is actually a hedge ("might not be accurate"); undocumented
episode details smuggled in as fact.

## Marker's note

The essay grade is not the average of its parts. A paper with a strong thesis but thin evidence discipline
fails the course's core value; return to the rubric (rubric 4 in `rubrics.md`) and grade the evidence
handling first, then the argument.
