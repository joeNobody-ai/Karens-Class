# Homework 15 — The capstone: a defended reading

**Due:** the final session (Module 15) · **Length:** 3–4 pages · **Weight:** the capstone (15% of the course grade)

## Task

This is the capstone: a defended cultural analysis of a single text, read with the course's toolkit.

1. **Choose one text.** One episode, one scene (as documented in the published summary or a reference
   card), or one run of reference-site cards. State it precisely — which episode, which fact.
2. **State a plain claim.** One sentence, arguable, specific, unmistakably yours. If you cannot say it in
   one sentence, it is not a claim yet.
3. **Read it with at least three course concepts** (e.g., Hall's decoding positions, Bourdieu's
   distinction, Gray's double burden, Durkheim's effervescence, Boym's nostalgia, hooks's gaze). Each
   concept must do *distinct* work on your text — not three names for the same observation.
4. **Separate the tiers.** Mark every documented fact as documented (title, air date, summary) and every
   reading of yours as interpretation. Keep them visibly apart throughout.
5. **Name an honest limit.** One thing your text *cannot* show, about itself or about the world — named
   precisely, not hedged.
6. **Argue the stakes.** Why your claim matters — what follows if you are right.
