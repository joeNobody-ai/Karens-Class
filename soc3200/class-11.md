# Module 11 — Health Inequality

**Big question.** Why does the gradient in health follow the gradient in class?

**Where we start.** We have followed the ladder through money, taste, race, gender, family, and work. Now
we follow it into the body. The episode *Final Words* sets a finals-week panic against a more serious
crisis — the published summary states the crisis is not named in the summary, but reframes what matters —
and the throwback *If I Should Die Before I Wake* faces mortality directly. The claim this week is
precise: health does not just fail at the bottom; it improves at every step up, following the gradient
that class already drew.

## Sociological frame

1. **The health gradient** (Marmot, 2004): the finding that health improves at *every* step up the
   socioeconomic ladder, not only at the bottom.
2. **Fundamental cause theory** (Link & Phelan, 1995): the claim that socioeconomic status is a
   "fundamental cause" of disease because it grants flexible resources — money, knowledge, power,
   prestige — that protect health against *whatever* the current threat is.
3. **Stress and status**: the mechanism by which position "gets under the skin" through the chronic
   stress of low control and high demand, embodied over time.

## Discussion questions

1. In the Ep 5 scene, what does the crisis reframe — and does the reframing itself follow the class
   gradient?
2. Why does fundamental cause theory say resources beat behaviour as an explanation? Give the mechanism.
3. Where in the throwback do you see *control* and *demand*, and how would each bear on health?
4. Can a single scene show a *gradient*, or only a point on it? What is the difference?
5. What would a researcher need to measure to test "stress gets under the skin" as a causal mechanism?

## Instructor notes

**If students reach for individual behaviour ("they just need to eat better").** Do not dismiss it; run
it through fundamental cause theory: who has the resources to change behaviour, and why does the gradient
persist *even when* behaviour is equal? The theory is the answer, not the scold.

**If a student is personally affected by the material.** Hold the room's norms: no one is asked to share,
silence is allowed, and the crisis in the episode is treated as a sociological datum, never as a prompt
for disclosure.
