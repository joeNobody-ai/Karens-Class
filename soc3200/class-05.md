# Module 5 — Class Inside Black America

**Big question.** What does stratification look like inside a group that is itself stratified against?

**Where we start.** Everything so far has treated the ladder as one national ranking. This week we zoom
into a harder question: Black America is not a single position on that ladder — it is itself stratified.
We return to Ep 3's status competition and to *Whitley's Last Supper*, and we read them for a second,
intraracial ladder: the tension between inherited standing and scholarship, between the "bougie" and the
working route through a historically Black college. Frazier's account of the Black bourgeoisie is our
anchor text for a stratification that runs *inside* the group.

## Sociological frame

1. **The Black middle class**: the stratum within Black America defined by occupation, education, and
   income, whose position is shaped by internal mobility *and* external racial constraint at once.
2. **Bougie versus scholarship**: a vernacular axis that names a real intragroup status tension — between
   position read as inherited or performed, and a route carried by need-based aid and work.
3. **Intraracial class**: stratification that runs inside a racial group, distinct from (but entangled
   with) the group's position relative to other groups.
4. **Frazier**: E. Franklin Frazier's account (*Black Bourgeoisie*, 1957) of a class torn between
   aspiration and marginality, performing a status it cannot fully secure.

## Discussion questions

1. Where in the Ep 3 tension beat do you see the *national* ladder, and where the *intraracial* one?
2. Is "bougie versus scholarship" a documented fact of the show, an interpretation, or a hypothesis? What
   would settle it?
3. What does Frazier's Black bourgeoisie explain about a character's performance that "class" alone
   cannot?
4. Can a group be privileged *within* and subordinated *without* at the same time? Show it in one scene.
5. What would a researcher have to measure to establish that an intraracial gap exists — and what would
   they risk missing?

## Instructor notes

**If the room flinches at discussing class inside Black America.** Name the difficulty directly: the
conversation is hard because it is real, not because it is forbidden. Hold to the norms — no one is a
spokesperson, and definitions come from Frazier and the cited texts, not from any student's life.

**If a student reduces the tension to "rich vs. poor."** Push for precision: standing is not the same as
money, and the bougie/scholarship axis is about *source* and *performance*, not just amount. Ask them to
re-map the scene with that distinction.
