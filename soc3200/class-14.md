# Module 14 — Global Inequality and Diaspora

**Big question.** How does the ladder look different once you draw it across borders?

**Where we start.** Every ladder so far has been drawn within one country. This week we draw it across
borders and find a different shape: a world-system in which some places are core and others periphery,
and in which migration itself is stratified — open to the rich, gated for the poor. We return to Ep 7's
diaspora argument, where the talent show becomes a debate about African and diaspora identity, and to the
throwback *A World Alike*, which took the same question abroad decades earlier.

## Sociological frame

1. **Global stratification**: the ranking of countries and regions into a world-wide order of wealth and
   power.
2. **The world system** (Wallerstein, 1974): the capitalist world-economy divided into core, periphery,
   and semi-periphery, whose positions are relational — one region's wealth is another's extraction.
3. **Diaspora**: a dispersed population linked across borders by origin, memory, and ongoing connection.
4. **Migration**: movement across borders that is itself stratified — differently open to the rich and
   the poor, the skilled and the unskilled.

## Discussion questions

1. In the Ep 7 scene, is the diaspora argument about culture, about position, or both? Anchor it.
2. What does "core and periphery" explain about the argument that "we're all the same people" cannot?
3. Who gets to cross a border in these scenes, and what does that movement cost each of them?
4. Is diaspora a *bond* or a *ranking* — or a bond that carries a ranking inside it? Defend your answer.
5. What would a global measure of inequality show that a national one hides — and vice versa?

## Instructor notes

**If students collapse "global inequality" into "poor countries vs. rich countries."** Push to the
relational claim: the world-system says the positions are *connected*, not just different. Ask what the
wealth of the core has to do with the periphery.

**If the room treats diaspora as purely cultural.** Route it back: diaspora is also a *position* — who
moved, who was moved, and who can move back. Ask them to find the position inside the identity.
