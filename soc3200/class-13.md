# Module 13 — Elites, Power, and Institutional Reproduction

**Big question.** Who runs the institution, and how does running it reproduce itself?

**Where we start.** We have studied the ladder's rungs; now we study its top. The season-closing episode
*A Gift & The Curse* puts the college's future in the hands of the people who govern it, and the throwback
*A Rock, a River, a Lena* returns to the same question of who steers and who inherits the steering. This
week the object is not the climbers but the gatekeepers: how a small group runs an institution, and how
running it keeps reproducing the same kind of people at the top.

## Sociological frame

1. **Elites** (Mills, 1956): the small groups at the top who command disproportionate resources and
   authority.
2. **Power** (Weber, 1922): the capacity to realize one's will even against resistance.
3. **Institutional reproduction**: the ways an institution's rules, boards, and pipelines keep recruiting
   and rewarding the same kind of people.
4. **The board**: the governing body that holds an institution's purse and direction — the concrete site
   where elite power is exercised.

## Discussion questions

1. In the Ep 10 scene, who is actually deciding the college's future — and who is not in the room?
2. What does Weber's definition of power capture that "influence" or "authority" would miss?
3. How does a board reproduce itself — through whom it appoints, funds, and listens to? Anchor one
   mechanism.
4. Is "the board runs the college" a documented fact, an interpretation, or a hypothesis? What would
   change the tier?
5. What would a researcher need to show to prove the institution *reproduces* its elite rather than
   merely *employing* one?

## Instructor notes

**If students imagine elites as villains.** Redirect to mechanism, not motive: the question is *how* the
loop runs, not whether the people in it are good or bad. Ask them to name the pipeline, not the villain.

**If the room says "the students run it" because they organise.** Hold both true: organizing is power too.
Ask who holds the *deciding* power when the crisis resolves, and how that differs from *protesting* power.
