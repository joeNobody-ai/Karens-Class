# Module 10 — Work, Labor, and the Economy

**Big question.** Who does the work, who gets the pay, and who gets to choose?

**Where we start.** Stratification is not only about who owns — it is about who works, on what terms, with
what security. We return to Ep 9's work-and-money beats, where students scramble for money while the
institution's own workers face the budget crisis, and to the throwback *Take This Job and Love It*, where
a job hunt makes the terms of work explicit. The question this week is not "do you have a job" but "what
kind of job, on whose terms, with what power to walk away."

## Sociological frame

1. **Labor markets**: the arenas where work is bought and sold, whose structure sets who gets which job
   at what pay.
2. **Precarity** (Standing, 2011): the condition of insecure, contingent work that shifts risk from the
   employer onto the worker.
3. **The gig economy**: work organized as discrete, platform-mediated tasks rather than stable
   employment, with little security or benefit.
4. **Deskilling** (Braverman, 1974): the reorganization of work that strips skill and discretion from
   jobs, cheapening the labour that does them.

## Discussion questions

1. In the Ep 9 scene, who chooses the work and who is chosen by it? Anchor each claim.
2. What does "precarity" name that "unemployment" misses? Find it in the scene.
3. Is the money scramble in Ep 9 the gig economy, or is it something older? What turns on the answer?
4. Does *Take This Job and Love It* show work that *adds* skill or work that *strips* it? Where?
5. Who has the power to walk away from a job in these scenes — and what does walking away cost?

## Instructor notes

**If students romanticize "hard work" as the whole story.** Ask what *kind* of work, on whose terms, with
what power to leave. The module's point is that the *terms* of work are themselves stratified, not that
effort is irrelevant.

**If the room confuses "the gig economy" with "any side hustle."** Tighten it: the gig economy is a
*structure* (platform-mediated, benefit-free, task-based), not any second job. Ask them to test whether
the scene's work fits the definition.
