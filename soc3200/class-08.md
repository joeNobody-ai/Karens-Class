# Module 8 — Gender and Stratification

**Big question.** Why does gender sort people into different rungs of the same ladder?

**Where we start.** The same institution, the same class positions — and yet gender sorts people into
different rungs. We return to Ep 7's pageant beat, where Hazel runs for Miss Gilbert Hall, and to the
throwback *Mr. Hillman*, a pageant episode from the original run. Two pageants, decades apart, both
asking the same question: what is being ranked when a woman is ranked, and who sets the terms?

## Sociological frame

1. **Gender stratification**: the ranking of groups by gender, with unequal access to resources,
   authority, and life chances.
2. **The second shift** (Hochschild, 1989): the unpaid domestic and care labour that falls
   disproportionately to women after the paid workday ends.
3. **Occupational segregation**: the uneven distribution of women and men across occupations, which
   depresses pay and status in feminized work.

## Discussion questions

1. What exactly does the Miss Gilbert Hall contest rank — and what is excluded from the ranking?
2. Is the pageant a *gender* ranking, a *class* ranking, or both? Anchor your answer in the scene.
3. How does the second shift operate at a college, where no one is a "housewife" yet? Find it.
4. In *Mr. Hillman*, which gender is being ranked, and does the title itself tell you anything? Cite it.
5. What would have to change between 1988 and 2026 for you to conclude gender no longer sorts the rungs?

## Instructor notes

**If a student says "things are totally different now."** Do not argue; hand them the two pageants and
ask them to name one thing that is *exactly the same* across the three decades. Sameness is the datum.

**If the room treats gender stratification as "a women's topic."** Reframe: the question is how *a ladder*
gets gendered — which is a question about everyone on it, including the men being ranked and the men
doing the ranking.
