# Module 1 — What Stratification Is and Why It Persists

**Big question.** Why does inequality survive every effort to reduce it?

**Where we start.** We open not with definitions but with a problem that will not go away: a college in a
budget crisis, and a group of students who organise in response. The 2026 episode *Romancing the Unknown*
makes the crisis visible, and the throwback *Take This Job and Love It* shows the same community thinking
about work and money decades earlier. Both are screens on which a ladder is visible before we have words
for it.

## Sociological frame

1. **Stratification**: the durable ranking of groups into layers with unequal access to valued resources,
   rewards, and life chances.
2. **Life chances** (Weber, 1922): the probability a person has of obtaining the goods, positions, and
   security a society offers, given where they sit in the ranking.
3. **Persistence**: the empirical tendency of inequality to reproduce its own shape across time and
   generations rather than converge.
4. **Structure**: the relatively stable social arrangements — institutions, rules, distributions — that
   shape individual outcomes independently of any one person's intentions.

## Discussion questions

1. Where in the Ep 9 scene do you see ranking, and where do you see people pushing back against it?
2. Is the budget crisis a fact of the institution or a fact of the people in it — and what turns on that
   distinction?
3. What would have to change for the ladder in *Take This Job and Love It* to look the same as the ladder
   in Ep 9? What would have to change for it to look different?
4. Which of the four concepts — stratification, life chances, persistence, structure — does the scene make
   hardest to see, and why?
5. Is "inequality persists" a documented fact, an interpretation, or a hypothesis? Defend your answer.

## Instructor notes

**If a student retells the whole episode instead of reading a scene.** Interrupt gently: "Give me one
thirty-second moment, and tell me what is ranked in it." Retelling is the room's default; redirect to the
anchor every time.

**If someone says inequality is "just natural" or "just human nature."** Do not argue. Ask what evidence
would settle it, then note that the question of persistence — not existence — is exactly the course's
opening problem, and that we will test the "natural" claim all term.
