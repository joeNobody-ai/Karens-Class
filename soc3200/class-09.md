# Module 9 — The Family and Intergenerational Mobility

**Big question.** How much of your place in the ladder was set before you were born?

**Where we start.** Every theory so far has assumed individuals climbing a ladder. This week we ask how
much of the climb was already decided at birth — by family, by inheritance, by the position you were
handed. The episode *Insomnia and the Daydreamer* brings the original cast (the "aunties") back into the
present, making the family's class history visible in its children, and the throwback *Whitley's Last
Supper* shows the same family's money at its source. Inheritance here is not just a will; it is
everything handed down.

## Sociological frame

1. **Intergenerational mobility**: the movement of individuals up or down the ladder relative to their
   parents' position.
2. **Inheritance**: the transfer of wealth, position, and advantage across generations — through assets,
   networks, and dispositions, not only through wills.
3. **Family and class**: the family as the primary site where class position is produced, transmitted,
   and naturalized.
4. **The aunties**: the original-cast characters who return in the revival, carrying the family's class
   history into the present generation.

## Discussion questions

1. In the Ep 6 scene, what does an "auntie" transmit to the next generation that is *not* money? Name it.
2. Is Deborah's position inherited, earned, or both? What in the scene would settle it?
3. How does *Whitley's Last Supper* show inheritance happening at a table, without anyone signing a will?
4. What is the difference between absolute and relative mobility — and why does only one of them tell you
   whether the ladder itself is fair?
5. "How much was set before you were born" — is that a question a scene can answer, or only a dataset?
   What would the data need?

## Instructor notes

**If students reduce inheritance to "money from parents."** Broaden it explicitly: name the non-monetary
transfers (networks, dispositions, a surname, a way of speaking) and ask which of them the scene actually
shows.

**If someone claims "everyone earns what they have."** Do not argue; ask what a mobility *measurement*
would look like and what it would find — then let the module's question ("set before you were born") do
the reframing.
