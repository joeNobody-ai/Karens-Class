# Session 3 — Culture

**Big question.** What holds a community together — and who sets the rules?

**Where we start.** Homecoming. A campus turns itself inside out for a weekend: step routines, a
headlining performer, alumni returning, and underneath it a marriage quietly falling apart. If culture is
the shared way of life of a group, homecoming is culture made visible.

## Sociological frame

1. **Culture**: the shared beliefs, values, norms, and material objects of a group — the "software" a
   community runs on. It is learned, shared, and mostly invisible to the people inside it.
2. **Subculture**: a group inside the larger culture with its own norms — here, a historically Black
   college inside American higher education, with a language and calendar of its own.
3. **Norms and values**: values are what a group says is good; norms are the rules it actually enforces.
   Homecoming enforces a lot without anyone saying the rules aloud.
4. **Collective effervescence** (Durkheim): the energy a group feels when it does something together —
   the feeling that the whole is more than the people in the room.

## The 75 minutes

| Min | What |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | **Screen the step routine.** Ask what the room saw — write the answers, then hold them as data about norms |
| 10–24 | **Define culture.** Material and non-material culture; values versus norms; the difference between what a group says and what it enforces |
| 24–38 | **Homecoming as ritual.** What the ritual is *for*: solidarity, belonging, boundary. What it hides: the cracking marriage, the labour that organises it |
| 38–52 | **Subculture and boundary.** What marks Hillman as its own culture — and what marks the line between who is inside it and who is outside |
| 52–64 | **Pair drill.** Each pair picks one norm visible in the scene and names the value it enforces, the boundary it draws, and who polices it |
| 64–75 | **Close and assign.** Why culture is the course's quietest and largest concept. Assign homework 3 |

## Discussion questions

1. What is "a Black college" in this episode — a place, a culture, or an argument?
2. Name a norm the scene enforces without anyone stating it.
3. Who does the work of making homecoming happen, and who gets the credit?
4. What does the ritual reveal that its participants would rather keep hidden?
5. What is a norm in your own life that you only notice when someone breaks it?

## Instructor notes

**If the room treats culture as "high culture" (museums, opera).** Redirect to the sociological sense:
culture is everything a group shares, from a step routine to a way of greeting. The step *is* culture —
no scare quotes.

**If someone says the step is "just a dance."** Ask what would be lost if it were removed from homecoming.
What a ritual protects shows in what breaks when it is gone — that is how you know it is not decoration.

**If a student raises class inside the culture.** Good instinct; note that the show itself makes status
competition visible at homecoming, and flag it for Module 7 (stratification) rather than resolving it here.
