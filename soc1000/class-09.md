# Session 9 — Gender and Sexuality

**Big question.** How is gender done — and what does a pageant show about it?

**Where we start.** A student runs for Miss Gilbert Hall the way another character did in 1988 — and
around the pageant, the season's romance scripts are being rewritten in real time. Gender is not a thing
you *are*; it is a thing you *do*, and a pageant is that doing on a public stage.

## Sociological frame

1. **Doing gender** (West and Zimmerman): gender is not a fixed trait but an ongoing performance —
   something accomplished in interaction, held to a standard, and judged.
2. **Gender scripts**: the shared expectations for how a man or a woman should behave in a given scene
   (a date, a pageant, a breakup). Scripts are learned, and they can be rewritten.
3. **Gender stratification**: the unequal distribution of power and resources along gender lines —
   who is watched, who is judged, who does the invisible work.
4. **Intersectionality (intro)**: gender never operates alone; it is always shaped by race, class, and
   other positions at once.

## Discussion questions

1. What is the difference between "being" a gender and "doing" one — in a pageant specifically?
2. Name a gender script you follow without thinking. Where did you learn it, and who enforces it?
3. What changed in the romance scripts between 1988 and 2026, and what stayed the same?
4. Who does the invisible work in the scene — and who gets the visible credit?
5. Why does gender never operate alone? Give an example where race or class changes how gender plays out.

## Instructor notes

**If a student says gender is "just biology."** Separate the physical from the social: whatever biology
supplies, the *meaning* of gender — the scripts, the stratification, the pageant — is social and varies
across time and place. That variation is the evidence for construction.

**If the room treats the pageant as trivial.** It is one of the show's most sociological objects: a public
competition in which gender, class, and colour are all on display and being scored at once. Take it
seriously and the room will follow.

**If a student raises colourism again.** Good — this is where Module 8 and Module 9 meet: the pageant
grades gender *and* skin tone in the same scorecard. That is intersectionality in one image.
