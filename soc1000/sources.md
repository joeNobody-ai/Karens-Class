# Sources

Two kinds of citation here. **Books and articles** are cited by author, title and year — no links,
because links to paywalled literature rot fastest. **The show and its record** are cited through the
reference project's documented pages, which carry their own sources and label every claim by tier.

> **Before you teach with any of these:** links and statistics age quickly. Re-check every figure you put
> on a board before the term starts, and re-run the source check in the parent project.

## Core text

- OpenStax, *Introduction to Sociology 3e* (Rice University, 2021) — the course's core textbook. Free and
  open access (search "OpenStax introduction to sociology").
- C. Wright Mills, *The Sociological Imagination* (1959), ch. 1 — the course's founding reading.

## Concept sources (cited by author, title, year)

- Émile Durkheim, *The Elementary Forms of Religious Life* (1912) — ritual and collective effervescence
  (Module 3).
- Erving Goffman, *The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life* (1959) and *Stigma* (1963) — the self,
  stigma, and the label (Modules 6, 13).
- Candace West and Don H. Zimmerman, "Doing Gender" (1987) — Module 9.
- Michael Omi and Howard Winant, *Racial Formation in the United States* (3rd ed., 2014) — Module 8.
- W. E. B. Du Bois, *The Souls of Black Folk* (1903), ch. 1 — the earliest statement of the perspective
  this course teaches.
- Marcel Mauss, *The Gift* (1925) — the gift and reciprocity (Module 14).
- David A. Snow and Robert D. Benford, "Ideology, Frame Resonance, and Participant Mobilization" (1988) —
  Module 13.
- Geneva Smitherman, *Black Talk* (1994) and Rickford &amp; Rickford, *Spoken Soul* (2000) — the language
  reference (see `lingo.md`).

## The shows as sources

Episode facts used throughout the course (titles, season and episode numbers, air dates, credits, cast,
and the published episode summaries) come from the reference project's documented pages, which cite their
own sources and label every claim by tier:

- Reference site (Netflix-style browse view): <https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/>
- Text version: <https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/>
- The tiered vocabulary reference: <https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/lingo.html>

**Attribution and use.** *A Different World* and its characters and episodes are the property of their
rights holders. This course teaches *about* the shows: it reproduces no scripts, no stills, and no
footage, and it summarises only what the shows' own published episode summaries state. Students are
directed to watch through legitimate services or library holdings, verified locally by the instructor.

*Sources file maintained by hand; see the parent project's `scripts/check_sources.py` and the upper-division
course's `scripts/make_sources.py` for the automated link-checking machinery.*
