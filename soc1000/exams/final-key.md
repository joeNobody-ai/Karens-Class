# Final key and grading rubric — INSTRUCTOR MATERIAL

**Do not distribute to students.** Model answers here are *standards*, not scripts: a strong student will
take a different route and should be rewarded for it.

---

## Part A (4 × 5 = 20 points) — what each full-credit answer contains

**A1 — Structure and agency.**
2 pts: the structure/agency distinction stated correctly (structure = the arrangements we are born into;
agency = what we choose inside them). 2 pts: one mechanism the claim identifies (individual decision) and
one it misses (inherited resources, segregated opportunity, labour market structure). 1 pt: a concrete,
answerable piece of evidence that would move the argument.

**A2 — Doing gender.**
2 pts: gender defined as an ongoing performance held to a standard, not a fixed trait. 2 pts: applied to a
specific scene with the standard and the enforcement named. 1 pt: the scene described as the student's own
observation (no fabricated dialogue).

**A3 — Movements and resources.**
2 pts: the difference between grievance and a movement (sustained, organized action). 2 pts: a frame and a
resource named from the chosen case (for the sit-in: a station, an audience, a decision-maker; for 2026:
organization, legitimacy, numbers). 1 pt: the cost to participants acknowledged.

**A4 — Race versus ethnicity.**
2 pts: race (an imposed, socially constructed category) distinguished from ethnicity (shared cultural
identity). 2 pts: the diaspora argument correctly identified as a contest over the *category* — who counts
as Black — with one documented detail. 1 pt: the "socially constructed but real" point handled without
denying consequences.

---

## Part B (20 points) — Scene analysis

| Points | Criterion | Notes for the marker |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | **Documented fact** | Title, air date, or published-summary detail accurate and clearly separated from interpretation. |
| 6 | **Three concepts** | Each does distinct work (not three words for one idea); used, not named. |
| 3 | **Structure and agency** | One of each identified in the scene. |
| 3 | **Honest limit** | The limit is about what a text cannot show, not a hedge ("it might be wrong"). |
| 3 | **Evidence discipline** | Tiers visible throughout; documented facts and readings never conflated. |
| 2 | **Sources** | At least two readings cited for something, not decoratively. |

**Marker's note:** reward answers that state the strongest case against their own reading. The skill this
course teaches is not confidence; it is the ability to hold a claim *and* its limits at once.

---

## Return-of-marks protocol

Return the final with one paragraph of feedback, and make the capstone (homework 15) part of that
conversation. The final and the capstone together answer one question: can the student now read the world
the way the course taught them to?
