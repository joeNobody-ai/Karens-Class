Answer **Part A** and **Part B**. Submit as one document. Cite your sources; for anything online, include

## Part A — Short answers (2 × 5 points = 10 points)

Answer **both**. 250 words each. A precise answer with a source beats a long one without.

### A1. The sociological imagination

Using C. Wright Mills's distinction between personal troubles and public issues, take **one** of the
following and restate it both ways: (a) a first-year student who cannot afford to stay enrolled; or (b)
a student who feels they do not belong on their campus. Name the structural arrangement that makes the
personal trouble a public issue, and say what question you would need to answer to test the public
version.

### A2. A documented fact and its interpretation

The original premiered on 24 September 1987; the revival premiered on 24 September 2026, 39 years later.
State what is **documented** about this pairing, what is a defensible **interpretation** of it, and what
cannot be settled with the evidence available. Then say why the distinction matters for how a sociologist
writes about a text.

## Part B — Concept application (10 points)

Answer **one** of the following. 400–500 words. Use **two** course concepts and **one** documented episode
fact (title and air date, or a published summary detail).

### B1. Culture and ritual

Using Ep 3 (*Center de Stage*, 2026-09-24), argue whether homecoming functions as a ritual in Durkheim's
sense. Identify one norm the ritual enforces, one boundary it draws, and one thing it conceals.

### B2. Deviance and the label

Using original S4E92 (*If I Should Die Before I Wake*, 1991-04-11) — the episode that put an HIV
disclosure at the centre of a sitcom — explain what a **label** is, who applied it in that historical
moment, and what the label cost the person it was applied to. Use the concept of stigma.

### B3. Stratification inside a community

Using Ep 9 (*Romancing the Unknown*, 2026-09-24), describe one way social class is visible inside a
community that calls itself one family. Distinguish wealth from income, and say what the budget crisis
reveals about the institution that a normal week conceals.
