# Session 11 — Education as an Institution

**Big question.** What is a college for, and who does it serve?

**Where we start.** Back at the budget crisis, but now asking what the *institution* is. A college is not
just a building full of classes; it is a set of rules, gateways, and interests — a machine for sorting
people into futures. When its money runs out, the machine becomes visible.

## Sociological frame

1. **Institution**: a stable, structured set of rules and roles that solves a recurring social problem —
   education, family, economy, religion. Institutions outlive the people in them.
2. **Education as institution**: schooling does more than teach; it sorts, credentials, and socializes.
   The "hidden curriculum" is the set of lessons taught by the institution's structure, not its syllabus.
3. **Hidden curriculum**: the unwritten rules — deference, punctuality, who speaks — that school teaches
   alongside the written ones.
4. **Credentialing**: the degree as a gate — a signal to employers that sorts people whether or not it
   measures what they learned.

## Discussion questions

1. What does a college do for a student that a set of books cannot?
2. What is the "hidden curriculum" of your own institution? Who does it favour?
3. Is a degree a measure of learning or a signal of something else — and to whom?
4. What does a budget crisis reveal about who an institution actually serves?
5. Why do institutions outlive the people who built them — and the people who hate them?

## Instructor notes

**If a student says "college is just job training."** Push: if that were all it was, the liberal-arts
core would not exist. But take the claim seriously — credentialing is real, and the course is not here to
flatter the institution it sits inside.

**If the room is cynical about the HBCU's value.** Keep both true at once: the institution is a
credentialing gate *and* a community that confers belonging and identity in a way a generic campus does
not. That double nature is the sociological point, not a flaw.

**If someone raises funding disparities by race.** Good — that is documented, checkable, and belongs to
Module 7's data habit. Invite them to bring a number next week (the homework asks for one).
