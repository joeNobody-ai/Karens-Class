# Session 8 — Race and Ethnicity

**Big question.** Is race something you are, or something you are assigned?

**Where we start.** The episode with the heaviest load: a talent show, an argument over African and
diaspora identity, and a title that quotes 1987 back at itself. Race is real in its consequences and
invented in its categories — and this episode puts that tension on stage.

## Sociological frame

1. **Race as social construction**: race is not a biological fact; it is a category people *made* — and
   keep making — with real, material consequences. "Socially constructed" does not mean "not real."
2. **Ethnicity**: a shared cultural identity — language, ancestry, nation, religion — distinct from the
   racial category imposed on a group.
3. **Racial formation** (Omi and Winant): the process by which racial categories are created, inhabited,
   and changed — never finished, always contested.
4. **Colourism**: stratification *within* a racial group by skin tone — proof that the category itself has
   an internal hierarchy.

## Discussion questions

1. If race is socially constructed, why are its consequences so material?
2. What is the difference between race and ethnicity in this episode?
3. Who gets to define who belongs to "the Black experience" — and who is written out when it is defined tightly?
4. Where have you seen colourism operate, inside a community you know?
5. What does it mean to say a category is "never finished"?

## Instructor notes

**If a student says "race isn't real, so we should just stop using it."** The consequences are real even
where the biology is not — you cannot wish away a category that organizes housing, policing, and life
chances. The question is not whether to *stop* using it but how to *see* it.

**If the room is nervous about saying the word "race."** Model the discipline: the category is the object
of study, named in the third person, never performed, never asked of any student as a spokesperson. The
vocabulary protocol in the instructor guide applies here in full.

**If a student raises colourism personally.** Do not make them the example. Move to the concept — colourism
is documented in the literature and visible in the show — and thank them without extracting testimony.
