# Session 7 — Social Stratification and Class

**Big question.** Why do some students have more — and why does it matter?

**Where we start.** Hillman's money runs out. A student is already working to make rent while another
never thinks about it. Stratification is the ranked arrangement of people into layers — and a campus,
for all its talk of community, is one of the clearest places to see it.

## Sociological frame

1. **Stratification**: the systematic ranking of groups in a society, distributing resources and life
   chances unevenly. It is a *structure*, not a pile of individual differences.
2. **Social class**: a position in that ranking, defined by wealth, income, education, occupation — and
   by the habits and expectations that come with it.
3. **Wealth versus income**: income is what flows in; wealth is what you already have. The gap between
   them is the deepest part of American stratification and the least visible in a paycheck.
4. **Mobility**: movement up or down the ladder — and the question of how much of it is actually open.

| Min | What |

## Discussion questions

1. What is the difference between a student who is *poor* and a student who is *broke for the month*?
2. How can two people earn the same amount and still be in different classes?
3. Where do you see class at work inside a community that talks about itself as one family?
4. Is mobility a matter of effort, structure, or both? What would you need to know to answer?
5. What does a budget crisis reveal about an institution that a normal week conceals?

## Instructor notes

**If a student says "inequality is just how the world works."** Separate description from explanation:
describing that it works that way is not explaining *why* it works that way. Sociology exists for the
second question.

**If the room slides into blaming individuals.** Keep the frame structural: a student's money problems
are the same event as a college's budget crisis seen from two heights. The structure produces both.

**If someone raises race and class together.** Good; note they intersect but are not the same, and flag
intersectionality for Module 8 and Module 9. Do not collapse one into the other.
