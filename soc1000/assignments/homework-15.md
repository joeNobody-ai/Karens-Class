# Homework 15 — The capstone: explain a concept to a non-major

**Due:** with the final · **Length:** 1 page (or 2–3 minutes spoken) · **Weight:** 10% of the course grade

## Task

The capstone is a test of the course's deepest skill: **public-facing writing.** Explain one sociological
concept to someone who has never taken a sociology course — a friend, a parent, a younger sibling — so
clearly that they could use it themselves.

1. Pick **one concept** from the term.
2. Write (or say) a one-page explanation that includes:
   - **The concept in one plain sentence** — no jargon.
   - **A concrete example** — a scene from *A Different World*, or something from ordinary life.
   - **Why it matters** — what changes in how a person sees the world once they know it.
   - **One thing it cannot do** — an honest limit, so the reader does not overclaim it.
3. If spoken: same four parts, in 2–3 minutes.

## Sources

- The concept as taught this term, and the reference site: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/

## Criteria (out of 20, scaled to course weight)

| Points | For |
|---|---|
| 5 | The one-sentence definition is actually plain (a non-major could repeat it) |
| 5 | The example is concrete and carries the concept, not just decorates it |
| 4 | "Why it matters" is argued with a real consequence |
| 3 | The honest limit is about the concept, not a hedge |
| 3 | No jargon used as a crutch; documented facts and interpretation kept apart |
