Homework 9 — Front stage, back stage

Due: start of Session 10 · Length: 1.5 pages · Weight: counts in the homework average

Task

Goffman's dramaturgy, applied to a room nobody can leave.

  1. Map the geography of 2026 Episode 8 (I Choose Chaos, the tornado warning) and original S5E99 (In the Eye of the Storm, the hurricane). For each: who is present, what exits exist, who controls them.
  2. Identify front stage and back stage for two characters in each text. Give the evidence for your reading — a specific behaviour that changes when the audience changes.
  3. The moment the performance stops. Find one beat in either text where a character stops managing the impression they were giving. Quote the content of what changes, or describe the behaviour if you have watched it. Say precisely what made the change possible.
  4. Negotiated order. In 200 words, argue whether the confinement caused the disclosure or merely permitted it. Use the texts as evidence and consider the alternative explanation: that the conversation was already overdue and something else would have triggered it.
  5. A refinement. Write two sentences on one way the storm scenes would be different if a character could simply walk out.

Sources

  • Ep 8 callback cards: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/ep08.html
  • Original: S5E99 In the Eye of the Storm (1991-10-17)
  • Erving Goffman, The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life (1959), ch. 1
  • Anselm L. Strauss, Negotiations (1978) — for negotiated order

Criteria (out of 10)

Points For
3 The geography maps are concrete (who, what exit, who controls it)
3 Front/back stage claims are supported by a change in behaviour, not by assertion
2 The performance-stops beat is identified precisely and explained
2 The causation argument considers the rival explanation seriously

Quality marker: the best submissions will notice that confinement does not create conflict — it removes the option of deferring it. Say that in your own words if you can.
