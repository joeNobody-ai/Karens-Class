Homework 3 — An ethnography of the ritual

Due: start of Session 4 · Length: 2 pages · Weight: counts in the homework average

Task

You are describing a ritual the way an ethnographer would: as a sequence of acts with participants, rules, and consequences.

  1. Inventory the televised homecoming. From 2026 Episode 3 (Center de Stage) and original S2E29 (A Stepping Stone), list every ritual element you can identify: procession, performance, dress, food, alumni presence, who speaks, who is silent. Two columns, side by side.
  2. Who does the work? Identify at least three kinds of labour that make each homecoming possible (paid, unpaid, visible, invisible).
  3. Boundaries. Find one moment in either text when someone is placed outside the circle. State the rule they broke, or the attribute that excluded them.
  4. The comparison. Write one paragraph: what does the 2026 version assume its audience already knows, that the 1988 version had to establish?

Sources

  • Ep 3 callback cards: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/ep03.html
  • Original: S2E29 A Stepping Stone (1988-12-01)
  • Émile Durkheim, The Elementary Forms of Religious Life (1912) — the concept of collective effervescence, in whatever translation your library holds
  • Optional, for one real-world comparison point you must cite: any documented account of an HBCU homecoming or step show (a university publication, a newspaper archive, an academic article). One source, properly cited, is worth more than three asserted from memory.

Criteria (out of 10)

Points For
3 The inventory is complete enough that a reader could picture the event
3 Labour identified by kind, not just listed (e.g. the work the band does before the show)
2 The boundary moment is specific and the rule is stated as a rule
2 The comparison paragraph makes a claim about audience knowledge and supports it

Evidence rule: the real-world comparison must be cited. If you cannot cite it, cut it.
