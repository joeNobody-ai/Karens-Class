Module 3 — Wealth, Income, and the Racial Wealth Gap

Big question. Why is the gap in wealth so much larger than the gap in income?

Where we start. Money shows up in the show as income — a character is "struggling financially" and looks for ways to make money — but the theory we need is about a different thing: wealth, the stock that accumulates and passes on. We return to Ep 9's money-pressure beats and pair them with the throwback Whitley's Last Supper, where class, family, and money meet at a dinner table. The goal this week is precision: income is not wealth, and the gap in one is not the gap in the other.

Sociological frame

  1. Wealth versus income: income is a flow — money received over a period; wealth is a stock — assets minus debts — and the stock is far more unequally distributed than the flow.
  2. The racial wealth gap: the persistent disparity in net worth between racial groups, rooted in accumulated advantage and disadvantage rather than in current earnings alone.
  3. Assets: the things a household owns that store value and can be transferred — a home, savings, investments, a business.
  4. Debt: the obligations that subtract from net worth and, when unequally priced, turn borrowing into a drag on accumulation rather than a ladder up.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–8 Open. A one-line distinction on the board — flow vs. stock — then screen the Ep 9 money-pressure beat
8–22 Income, precisely. Define income; ask what the scene shows us that income can and cannot explain
22–36 Wealth, precisely. Define wealth, assets, and debt; build a small household balance sheet on the board and ask what the scene's characters would show on it
36–50 The gap. Introduce the racial wealth gap and its scale; screen the Whitley's Last Supper dinner beat and ask what kind of money — flow or stock — is actually at stake at that table
50–63 Why the stock gap dwarfs the flow gap. Accumulation, inheritance, and the difference between earning and owning; preview Homework 3's data exercise
63–75 Close and assign. Assign Homework 3 (the data exercise); restate why measurement precision matters for the rest of the course

Discussion questions

  1. What does the Ep 9 scene let you see about income, and what about wealth is invisible in it?
  2. If two characters earn the same income, can one be far richer than the other? Build the balance sheet that makes it true.
  3. Why does the racial wealth gap outlast the racial income gap even when incomes converge? Think in stocks, not flows.
  4. In Whitley's Last Supper, is the money at the table documented, or is it your reading of the scene? Where is the line?
  5. Is "debt" always a disadvantage, or can it be a form of wealth-building — and for whom?

Instructor notes

If a student keeps saying "wealth" when they mean "income." This is the module's whole point. Correct it every single time and route the correction through the flow/stock distinction, not through shame.

If a student wants a single "the gap is X" number memorised. Remind the room that the figure is dated and sourced, that the data exercise next week asks them to find the current number themselves, and that the skill is reading a figure — source, year, denominator — not quoting one.
