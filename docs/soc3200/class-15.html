Module 15 — Capstone: Explaining Inequality

Big question. Can you build and defend a causal account of one inequality?

Where we start. The term ends where it began — with the question of why inequality persists — but now you have the tools to answer it. This module is yours: each of you presents a causal account of one inequality, built from the theories and the evidence discipline of the past fourteen weeks, and defends it against the room. The season-closing A Gift & The Curse and the throwback A Rock, a River, a Lena are the shared screen one last time: two endings that each tried to say what the college — and its ladder — would become.

Sociological frame

  1. Synthesis: bringing the course's theories and evidence together into a single coherent account.
  2. Causal argument: a claim about what produces an outcome, backed by mechanism and evidence — not merely correlation.
  3. Theory and evidence: the discipline of making a claim that a body of documented evidence can support or defeat.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–8 Open. Restate the capstone task and the rubric; one student volunteers to go first
8–22 Presentations, round one. Two students present their causal accounts (five minutes each); the room holds questions until each is done
22–36 Defence. The room probes each account — mechanism, evidence, and the honest limit of the claim
36–50 Presentations, round two. Two more students present and are probed on the same terms
50–63 The ending as evidence. Screen the season-close beat from Ep 10 and the A Rock, a River, a Lena beat; ask what each ending claims about the ladder's future, and whether the term's evidence supports it
63–75 Close and assign. Collect the final; run the ungraded self-assessment; assign Homework 15 (the written capstone, due with the final)

Discussion questions

  1. What is the difference between a correlation and a cause in the account you just heard? Pin it.
  2. Which single theory from the term does your account lean on hardest — and what does it force you to leave out?
  3. What is the honest limit of your causal account — the thing it cannot show?
  4. What does the season-closing episode claim about the future of the ladder, and is that claim documented, interpretation, or hypothesis?
  5. If you could add one measurement to your account, what would it be, and what would it prove?

Instructor notes

If a student's account is all mechanism and no evidence. Send them back to the tiers: which link is documented, which is interpretation, which is hypothesis? A causal account earns its confidence from that honesty, not from confidence of tone.

If the room is too gentle. The defence is the point of the exercise. Model one hard, fair question yourself ("what would falsify this?") and require each presenter to answer it before moving on.

Key terms

synthesis · causal argument · mechanism · evidence · falsifiability
