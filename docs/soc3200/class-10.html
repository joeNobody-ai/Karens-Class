Module 10 — Work, Labor, and the Economy

Big question. Who does the work, who gets the pay, and who gets to choose?

Where we start. Stratification is not only about who owns — it is about who works, on what terms, with what security. We return to Ep 9's work-and-money beats, where students scramble for money while the institution's own workers face the budget crisis, and to the throwback Take This Job and Love It, where a job hunt makes the terms of work explicit. The question this week is not "do you have a job" but "what kind of job, on whose terms, with what power to walk away."

Sociological frame

  1. Labor markets: the arenas where work is bought and sold, whose structure sets who gets which job at what pay.
  2. Precarity (Standing, 2011): the condition of insecure, contingent work that shifts risk from the employer onto the worker.
  3. The gig economy: work organized as discrete, platform-mediated tasks rather than stable employment, with little security or benefit.
  4. Deskilling (Braverman, 1974): the reorganization of work that strips skill and discretion from jobs, cheapening the labour that does them.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–8 Open. Pose the question: is a job freedom or a tether? Screen the money-scramble beat from Ep 9
8–22 Labor markets, defined. What sets who gets which job at what pay; apply to the scramble in the scene
22–36 Precarity and the gig. How risk has moved from employer to worker; ask which characters in the scene carry it
36–50 Deskilling. Screen the Take This Job and Love It beat; ask whether the jobs on offer add skill or strip it
50–63 Who gets to choose. The difference between choosing work and being chosen by it; test the distinction against both eras
63–75 Close and assign. Assign Homework 10; preview Module 11 (health), where the ladder gets under the skin

Discussion questions

  1. In the Ep 9 scene, who chooses the work and who is chosen by it? Anchor each claim.
  2. What does "precarity" name that "unemployment" misses? Find it in the scene.
  3. Is the money scramble in Ep 9 the gig economy, or is it something older? What turns on the answer?
  4. Does Take This Job and Love It show work that adds skill or work that strips it? Where?
  5. Who has the power to walk away from a job in these scenes — and what does walking away cost?

Instructor notes

If students romanticize "hard work" as the whole story. Ask what kind of work, on whose terms, with what power to leave. The module's point is that the terms of work are themselves stratified, not that effort is irrelevant.

If the room confuses "the gig economy" with "any side hustle." Tighten it: the gig economy is a structure (platform-mediated, benefit-free, task-based), not any second job. Ask them to test whether the scene's work fits the definition.

Key terms

labor markets · precarity · gig economy · deskilling · contingency
