Module 8 — Gender and Stratification

Big question. Why does gender sort people into different rungs of the same ladder?

Where we start. The same institution, the same class positions — and yet gender sorts people into different rungs. We return to Ep 7's pageant beat, where Hazel runs for Miss Gilbert Hall, and to the throwback Mr. Hillman, a pageant episode from the original run. Two pageants, decades apart, both asking the same question: what is being ranked when a woman is ranked, and who sets the terms?

Before class: viewing

Reference site for both, with sources: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/

Sociological frame

  1. Gender stratification: the ranking of groups by gender, with unequal access to resources, authority, and life chances.
  2. The second shift (Hochschild, 1989): the unpaid domestic and care labour that falls disproportionately to women after the paid workday ends.
  3. Occupational segregation: the uneven distribution of women and men across occupations, which depresses pay and status in feminized work.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–8 Open. Pose the question; screen the Miss Gilbert Hall beat from Ep 7
8–22 Gender stratification, defined. Distinguish it from sex and from individual bias; ask what the pageant ranks and what it cannot rank
22–36 Who sets the terms? Read the pageant's criteria as a ranking device; ask whose standard is being applied
36–50 The second shift and segregation. Screen the Mr. Hillman throwback; map how domestic labour and feminized work sort women into lower rungs even at the same college
50–63 Same ladder, different rungs. Compare the two pageants across three decades: what changed, what did not, and what that says about gender's staying power
63–75 Close and assign. Assign Homework 8; preview Module 9 (family and mobility)

Discussion questions

  1. What exactly does the Miss Gilbert Hall contest rank — and what is excluded from the ranking?
  2. Is the pageant a gender ranking, a class ranking, or both? Anchor your answer in the scene.
  3. How does the second shift operate at a college, where no one is a "housewife" yet? Find it.
  4. In Mr. Hillman, which gender is being ranked, and does the title itself tell you anything? Cite it.
  5. What would have to change between 1988 and 2026 for you to conclude gender no longer sorts the rungs?

Instructor notes

If a student says "things are totally different now." Do not argue; hand them the two pageants and ask them to name one thing that is exactly the same across the three decades. Sameness is the datum.

If the room treats gender stratification as "a women's topic." Reframe: the question is how a ladder gets gendered — which is a question about everyone on it, including the men being ranked and the men doing the ranking.

gender stratification · the second shift · occupational segregation · pageant
