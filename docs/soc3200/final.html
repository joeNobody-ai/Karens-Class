SOC 3200 — Social Stratification and Inequality — Final Examination

Session: Module 15 · Weight: 30% of the final grade · Format: take-home, open-book, cumulative · Total points: 100

Instructions

  • The evidence rule. Keep three kinds of claim apart throughout: documented (an episode title, air date, credit, or published summary — look-up-able), interpretation (a reading, labelled as yours), and hypothesis (a claim on the table for testing). Cite only the episodes listed in this course; do not invent dialogue, plot beats, or statistics. When you are unsure whether a detail is documented, say so and stick to what the published summary states.
  • AI note. You may use tools to help you find sources, check grammar, or brainstorm — but not to produce the analysis you submit. Every claim must be one you can defend, with a documented source, in the room.
  • Answer all of Part A and one question in Part B. Cite the original-series titles and air dates exactly as given in your course materials. The exam is cumulative: draw on any module of the term.

Part A — Short answers (5 × 12 points = 60)

A1. Exploitation and power. Distinguish Marx's exploitation from Weber's power. The published summary of Ep 10, A Gift & The Curse, states that major decisions are made about the college's future. Explain how that decision-making reveals both a class dimension and a power dimension, and where they diverge.

A2. The health gradient. Define the health gradient (Marmot) and fundamental cause theory (Link and Phelan), and state the mechanism — the flexible resources — by which status protects health. The published summary of Ep 5, Final Words, states that a serious crisis interrupts the finals panic and reframes what matters. Explain why a single scene can show only a point on the gradient, never the gradient itself.

A3. Place and opportunity hoarding. Define residential segregation, neighborhood effects, and opportunity hoarding (Tilly). The published summary of Ep 1, Irreconcilable Differences, states that Deborah starts her freshman year at Hillman. Read Hillman as a place: name the resources attached to the address, and explain how a group hoards them.

A4. The world system. Define global stratification and the world system (core/periphery), and explain why its positions are relational. The published summary of Ep 7 states that Kojo is caught up in arguments over African/diaspora identity. Explain how the argument carries a position inside the identity.

A5. Inheritance beyond the will. Define intergenerational mobility (absolute vs. relative) and inheritance understood as more than a will. The published summary of Ep 6, Insomnia and the Daydreamer, states that the "aunties" (original cast) arrive. Name two things an auntie transmits that are not money, and say which kind of mobility a single scene can and cannot show.

Part B — The causal account (40 points)

Build and defend a causal account of one inequality.

  1. State your causal claim in one sentence — what produces the outcome, and for whom.
  2. Give the mechanism, drawing on at least two theories from the course.
  3. Cite one documented episode fact as evidence, and anchor it to a specific screened scene.
  4. Name the honest limit of your account, and one measurement that would strengthen or break it.

Keep documented facts and interpretations visibly separate throughout.
