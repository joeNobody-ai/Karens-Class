Module 1 — What Stratification Is and Why It Persists

Big question. Why does inequality survive every effort to reduce it?

Where we start. We open not with definitions but with a problem that will not go away: a college in a budget crisis, and a group of students who organise in response. The 2026 episode Romancing the Unknown makes the crisis visible, and the throwback Take This Job and Love It shows the same community thinking about work and money decades earlier. Both are screens on which a ladder is visible before we have words for it.

Before class: viewing

Text Episode First aired Why this one
Revival (2026) Ep 9, Romancing the Unknown 2026 The published summary states Hillman faces a budget crisis and the students organise — a live case of structure and agency
Original (1989) S2E41, Take This Job and Love It 1989-03-16 A study of work and money at the same college, three decades earlier

Sociological frame

  1. Stratification: the durable ranking of groups into layers with unequal access to valued resources, rewards, and life chances.
  2. Life chances (Weber, 1922): the probability a person has of obtaining the goods, positions, and security a society offers, given where they sit in the ranking.
  3. Persistence: the empirical tendency of inequality to reproduce its own shape across time and generations rather than converge.
  4. Structure: the relatively stable social arrangements — institutions, rules, distributions — that shape individual outcomes independently of any one person's intentions.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–8 Open and orient. State the big question; screen the two- to three-minute budget-crisis beat from Ep 9 once, without commentary
8–22 Name what was seen. Cold-call descriptions of the scene; every observation must carry an anchor ("where did you see that?")
22–36 Define the four concepts. Stratification, life chances, persistence, structure — each in one sentence, each tied back to the screened scene
36–50 The throwback. Screen the work-and-money beat from Take This Job and Love It; compare the ladder of 1989 with the ladder of 2026
50–63 The persistence problem. Why has the ladder not gone away? Introduce the three tiers (documented / interpretation / hypothesis) and run a practice pass on the room's own claims
63–75 Close and assign. Restate the big question; assign Homework 1; preview Module 2 (Marx and Weber)

Discussion questions

  1. Where in the Ep 9 scene do you see ranking, and where do you see people pushing back against it?
  2. Is the budget crisis a fact of the institution or a fact of the people in it — and what turns on that distinction?
  3. What would have to change for the ladder in Take This Job and Love It to look the same as the ladder in Ep 9? What would have to change for it to look different?
  4. Which of the four concepts — stratification, life chances, persistence, structure — does the scene make hardest to see, and why?
  5. Is "inequality persists" a documented fact, an interpretation, or a hypothesis? Defend your answer.

Instructor notes

If a student retells the whole episode instead of reading a scene. Interrupt gently: "Give me one thirty-second moment, and tell me what is ranked in it." Retelling is the room's default; redirect to the anchor every time.

If someone says inequality is "just natural" or "just human nature." Do not argue. Ask what evidence would settle it, then note that the question of persistence — not existence — is exactly the course's opening problem, and that we will test the "natural" claim all term.
