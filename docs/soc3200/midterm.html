SOC 3200 — Social Stratification and Inequality — Midterm Examination

Released: after Module 7 · Due: before Module 9 · Weight: 20% of the final grade · Format: take-home, open-book · Total points: 100

Instructions

  • The evidence rule. Keep three kinds of claim apart throughout: documented (an episode title, air date, credit, or published summary — look-up-able), interpretation (a reading, labelled as yours), and hypothesis (a claim on the table for testing). Cite only the episodes listed in this course; do not invent dialogue, plot beats, or statistics. When you are unsure whether a detail is documented, say so and stick to what the published summary states.
  • AI note. You may use tools to help you find sources, check grammar, or brainstorm — but not to produce the analysis you submit. Every claim must be one you can defend, with a documented source, in the room.
  • Answer all of Part A and one question in Part B. Cite the original-series titles and air dates exactly as given in your course materials.

Part A — Short answers (5 × 12 points = 60)

Answer each in a short paragraph. Each question embeds a documented episode fact; build your answer on it and mark where you move from documented fact to interpretation.

A1. Stratification and life chances. Define stratification and Weber's life chances in one or two sentences each. The published summary of Ep 9, Romancing the Unknown, states that Hillman faces a budget crisis and the students organise. Explain how the crisis reveals a structure — a ranking that operates independently of any one student's intentions.

A2. Marx and Weber. Distinguish Marx's account of class from Weber's three dimensions (class, status, party). Then apply both to one beat of your choosing from Ep 9 or from the original Take This Job and Love It (S2E41, 1989-03-16): name what each lens makes visible and what it hides.

A3. Wealth versus income. Define wealth and income, and explain why the racial wealth gap is far larger than the racial income gap (think in stocks versus flows, assets versus debt). Anchor the distinction in a documented money-pressure fact: the published summary of Ep 2, O Captain! My Captain!, states that Rashida is struggling financially and looks for ways to make money.

A4. Distinction. Define Bourdieu's cultural capital, distinction, and habitus. The published summary of Ep 3, Center de Stage, states that Deborah tries to secure a headlining performer. Explain how that headliner fight can be read as class doing its work through taste — without naming money — and mark clearly where you are citing a documented fact and where you are offering an interpretation.

A5. Race and formation. Define racial formation (Omi and Winant) and distinguish it from racial stratification. The published summary of Ep 7 states that Kojo is caught up in arguments over African/diaspora identity. Explain how such an argument can be read as a moment of racial formation rather than merely a disagreement about culture.

Part B — Applied question (40 points)

Choose one beat from Ep 9 (Romancing the Unknown), Ep 3 (Center de Stage), or the original Whitley's Last Supper (S4E73, 1990-10-11). Read that beat through Marx, Weber, and Bourdieu in turn. Then argue which of the three dimensions — class, status, or culture — best explains the ranking you see, and state the honest limit of your account (what the beat cannot show). Keep documented facts and interpretations visibly separate throughout.
