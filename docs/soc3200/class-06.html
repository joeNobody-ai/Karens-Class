Module 6 — Education and Reproduction

Big question. How does schooling sort people into futures — and pass the sorting on?

Where we start. The college that has been our setting all term is now the object of study. A historically Black college is a ladder and a sorting machine: it lifts, and it also sorts who goes where afterward, and it passes that sorting to the next generation. We return to Ep 9's institutional crisis and to Take This Job and Love It to ask what a degree actually does — teach, or credential, or both — and whose advantage the institution quietly reproduces.

Sociological frame

  1. Reproduction: the process by which institutions pass advantage and disadvantage from one generation to the next, making an inherited position look earned.
  2. Credentialing (Collins, 1979): the use of degrees and certificates as a sorting and rationing device rather than — or in addition to — a measure of skill.
  3. Cultural capital (Bourdieu, 1986): the knowledge and dispositions schools reward, which privileged families transmit at home.
  4. Bourdieu and Passeron (Reproduction, 1977): the claim that schooling's hidden work is to legitimate inherited inequality as merit, so that the system's winners look self-made.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–8 Open. Pose the question: is Hillman a ladder up, a sorting machine, or both? Screen the student-organising beat from Ep 9
8–22 Reproduction, defined. Distinguish mobility (individuals move) from reproduction (the ranking itself persists); apply to the scene
22–36 Credentialing. Collins's question — does the degree measure skill or ration opportunity? Test it against Take This Job and Love It
36–50 Cultural capital at school. What do schools reward that is taught at home, not in class? Screen the throwback beat and read it for capital
50–63 The legitimate look of inheritance. Bourdieu and Passeron's harder claim: the system works best when the winners believe they earned it. Interrogate the scene for that belief
63–75 Close and assign. Assign Homework 6; flag the midterm is released next week (after Module 7)

Discussion questions

  1. In the Ep 9 organising beat, is the institution opening or closing? Anchor your answer.
  2. If a degree rations opportunity, what exactly is being rationed — and to whom?
  3. Does Take This Job and Love It show a degree as skill, as credential, or as both? Where?
  4. What does a school reward that a student could not have learned in class? Name one such thing from the scene.
  5. "Reproduction makes the inherited look earned." Is that a documented fact, an interpretation, or a hypothesis — and what would falsify it?

Instructor notes

If students insist schooling is a pure meritocracy. Do not lecture; hand them Collins's question and one scene, and ask them to find the sorting that is not about skill. Let the evidence do the work.

If the room confuses "reproduction" with "no one moves." Clarify sharply: individual mobility and structural reproduction can be true at once. The question is whether the ladder changes, not whether individuals climb it.
