SOC 3200 — Final Examination — Answer Key (Instructor Material)

Do not distribute to students.

Each question is marked for what a full-credit answer must contain, not for a single "correct" script. Award points for the presence of the required elements and for evidence discipline; deduct for fabrication and for tier-blurring. This is a cumulative exam: reward integration across modules.

Part A — 12 points each

A1. Exploitation and power

Points Full-credit answer must contain
3 Marx's exploitation (surplus appropriated by the class controlling production) and Weber's power (will realized against resistance) defined distinctly
3 The Ep 10 documented fact (major decisions about the college's future) anchored
3 The class dimension and the power dimension both identified in the decision-making
3 A statement of where the two dimensions diverge (not everything powerful is exploitative, and vice versa)

Common errors: using "power" and "exploitation" interchangeably; treating the decision as class-only or power-only; no anchor.

A2. The health gradient

Points Full-credit answer must contain
3 The gradient defined (health improves at every step up, not only at the bottom)
3 Fundamental cause theory defined, with the mechanism named (flexible resources: money, knowledge, power, prestige)
3 The Ep 5 documented fact anchored
3 The point/gradient distinction stated precisely: a scene shows a point; a dataset shows the gradient

Common errors: reducing the gradient to "poor people are sick"; naming the theory without its mechanism; claiming a single scene demonstrates the whole gradient.

A3. Place and opportunity hoarding

Points Full-credit answer must contain
3 Residential segregation, neighborhood effects, and opportunity hoarding each defined distinctly
3 The Ep 1 documented fact (Deborah starts freshman year at Hillman) anchored
3 Hillman read as a place — resources attached to the address (network, school, safety margin, price of entry) named
3 The hoarding mechanism explained (a group using its resources/networks to secure insiders' advantage)

Common errors: treating place as scenery; merging the three terms; naming no concrete resource.

A4. The world system

Points Full-credit answer must contain
3 Global stratification and the world system (core/periphery/semi-periphery) defined
3 The relational claim stated — one region's wealth is another's extraction, positions connected, not just different
3 The Ep 7 documented fact (Kojo in the diaspora argument) anchored
3 A position identified inside the identity argument (who moved, who was moved, who can move back)

Common errors: listing rich vs. poor places without relation; treating diaspora as purely cultural; no anchor.

A5. Inheritance beyond the will

Points Full-credit answer must contain
3 Intergenerational mobility defined, with absolute and relative mobility correctly distinguished
3 Inheritance defined as more than a will (networks, dispositions, a name, ways of moving)
3 The Ep 6 documented fact (the aunties arrive) anchored, and two non-monetary transmissions named
3 Which mobility a scene can show (absolute) and which it cannot (relative) correctly identified

Common errors: inheritance reduced to "money from parents"; conflating absolute and relative mobility; naming only one transmission or none.

Part B — The causal account — 40 points

Points Full-credit answer must contain
8 A plain, specific, falsifiable causal claim — unmistakably the student's, not "inequality exists"
8 A mechanism in the terms of at least two distinct theories from the course
8 One documented episode fact cited and anchored to a specific screened scene
8 The honest limit named (about the claim, not a hedge) plus one measurement that would strengthen or break it
8 Documented facts and interpretations visibly separated throughout

Common errors: a correlation dressed as a cause; one theory doing all the work (or two used as synonyms); evidence cited decoratively rather than carrying the claim; no honest limit; tier-blurring.

Marker's note. Part B is the course's capstone in miniature. The single most diagnostic thing is whether the student can say what would break their claim. A confident account with no falsifier is worth at most half the limit points regardless of how fluent it reads.
