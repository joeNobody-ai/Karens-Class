Module 13 — Elites, Power, and Institutional Reproduction

Big question. Who runs the institution, and how does running it reproduce itself?

Where we start. We have studied the ladder's rungs; now we study its top. The season-closing episode A Gift & The Curse puts the college's future in the hands of the people who govern it, and the throwback A Rock, a River, a Lena returns to the same question of who steers and who inherits the steering. This week the object is not the climbers but the gatekeepers: how a small group runs an institution, and how running it keeps reproducing the same kind of people at the top.

Before class: viewing

Text Episode First aired Why this one
Revival (2026) Ep 10, A Gift & The Curse 2026 The published summary states major decisions are made about the college's future — power exercised in the open
Original (1993) S6E141, A Rock, a River, a Lena 1993-07-02 The original series' own reckoning with who leads and who is left out of the leading

Reference site for both, with sources: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/

Sociological frame

  1. Elites (Mills, 1956): the small groups at the top who command disproportionate resources and authority.
  2. Power (Weber, 1922): the capacity to realize one's will even against resistance.
  3. Institutional reproduction: the ways an institution's rules, boards, and pipelines keep recruiting and rewarding the same kind of people.
  4. The board: the governing body that holds an institution's purse and direction — the concrete site where elite power is exercised.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–8 Open. Pose the question; screen the college's-future beat from Ep 10 and ask simply: who is deciding?
8–22 Power, defined. Weber's definition against the scene — who realizes their will, against whose resistance
22–36 Elites and the board. Who sits at the top, how they got there, and what the board does that a vote does not
36–50 Institutional reproduction. Screen the A Rock, a River, a Lena beat; trace how the institution recruits and rewards its own kind
50–63 Running it reproduces it. The loop: the rulers shape the rules that select the next rulers. Test the loop against both eras
63–75 Close and assign. Assign Homework 13; preview Module 14 (the ladder drawn across borders)

Discussion questions

  1. In the Ep 10 scene, who is actually deciding the college's future — and who is not in the room?
  2. What does Weber's definition of power capture that "influence" or "authority" would miss?
  3. How does a board reproduce itself — through whom it appoints, funds, and listens to? Anchor one mechanism.
  4. Is "the board runs the college" a documented fact, an interpretation, or a hypothesis? What would change the tier?
  5. What would a researcher need to show to prove the institution reproduces its elite rather than merely employing one?

Instructor notes

If students imagine elites as villains. Redirect to mechanism, not motive: the question is how the loop runs, not whether the people in it are good or bad. Ask them to name the pipeline, not the villain.

If the room says "the students run it" because they organise. Hold both true: organizing is power too. Ask who holds the deciding power when the crisis resolves, and how that differs from protesting power.

Key terms

elites · power · institutional reproduction · the board · gatekeeping
