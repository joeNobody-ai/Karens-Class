Session 11 — Education as an Institution

Big question. What is a college for, and who does it serve?

Where we start. Back at the budget crisis, but now asking what the institution is. A college is not just a building full of classes; it is a set of rules, gateways, and interests — a machine for sorting people into futures. When its money runs out, the machine becomes visible.

Before class: viewing

Text Episode First aired Why this one
Revival (2026) Ep 9, Romancing the Unknown (revisit) 2026-09-24 The budget crisis as a window into what the institution is for
Original (1989) S2E41, Take This Job and Love It 1989-03-16 Work and education, the same tension a generation earlier

Reference site for both, with sources: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/

Sociological frame

  1. Institution: a stable, structured set of rules and roles that solves a recurring social problem — education, family, economy, religion. Institutions outlive the people in them.
  2. Education as institution: schooling does more than teach; it sorts, credentials, and socializes. The "hidden curriculum" is the set of lessons taught by the institution's structure, not its syllabus.
  3. Hidden curriculum: the unwritten rules — deference, punctuality, who speaks — that school teaches alongside the written ones.
  4. Credentialing: the degree as a gate — a signal to employers that sorts people whether or not it measures what they learned.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–10 Screen the students organising. Ask: what is the college for in this scene — teaching, sorting, or surviving?
10–24 Define institution and education. Institutions as machines for solving recurring problems; what a college solves that a library cannot
24–38 The hidden curriculum. What Hillman teaches that is not on any syllabus. What every college teaches that way
38–52 Credentialing and the gate. The degree as a signal. Who is sorted where, and what the budget crisis does to the sorting
52–64 Pair drill. Each pair names one thing the institution does in the scene that a classroom alone could not do
64–75 Close and assign. Bridge to Module 12 (who controls the image of the institution). Assign homework 11

Discussion questions

  1. What does a college do for a student that a set of books cannot?
  2. What is the "hidden curriculum" of your own institution? Who does it favour?
  3. Is a degree a measure of learning or a signal of something else — and to whom?
  4. What does a budget crisis reveal about who an institution actually serves?
  5. Why do institutions outlive the people who built them — and the people who hate them?

Instructor notes

If a student says "college is just job training." Push: if that were all it was, the liberal-arts core would not exist. But take the claim seriously — credentialing is real, and the course is not here to flatter the institution it sits inside.

If the room is cynical about the HBCU's value. Keep both true at once: the institution is a credentialing gate and a community that confers belonging and identity in a way a generic campus does not. That double nature is the sociological point, not a flaw.

If someone raises funding disparities by race. Good — that is documented, checkable, and belongs to Module 7's data habit. Invite them to bring a number next week (the homework asks for one).
