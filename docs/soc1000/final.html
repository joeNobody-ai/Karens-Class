Final examination

SOC 1000 · Introduction to Sociology: Reading the Social World through A Different World Session: 15 (or your institution's examination period) · Weight: 30% of the course grade Total: 40 points

Two permitted formats — choose one and announce it at the start of term:

Format Time Materials
In-class (recommended) 2 hours Exhibits provided; no notes, no devices
Take-home 4 hours, single sitting Course readings and reference site permitted; citations required

Either way the paper is comprehensive and structured around transfer: can you do this work on material the course did not cover?

Part A — Short answers (4 × 5 = 20 points)

Answer all four. 200–250 words each.

A1. A student claims: "People are poor because they make bad choices." Assess the claim using the distinction between structure and agency. Name one mechanism the claim identifies, one it misses, and one piece of evidence that would move the argument forward.

A2. Define doing gender, and use it to explain one feature of a scene from the course (for example, the Miss Gilbert Hall pageant, or a date in Those Who Can...Cuff). Do not quote dialogue; describe the scene and mark it as your observation.

A3. Explain what a social movement needs beyond grievance, using either the sit-in in original S2E32 (Radio Free Hillman, 1989-01-05) or the student organising in Ep 9 (Romancing the Unknown, 2026-09-24). Name the frame and the resource.

A4. Distinguish race from ethnicity, and say which of the two is doing the work in the diaspora argument in Ep 7 — with one documented detail to support your answer.

Part B — Scene analysis (20 points)

Choose one scene from either series that the course did not spend a whole module on. Write 750 words reading it sociologically:

  1. One documented fact about the scene (title, air date, or a published-summary detail), kept clearly separate from what follows.
  2. Three concepts from the term, each doing distinct work.
  3. Structure and agency — identify one of each in the scene.
  4. One honest limit — something the scene cannot show you, no matter how well you read it.

Use at least two sources from the reading, and separate documented facts from your interpretations throughout.

Rules

  • Every empirical claim: source and year. Anything online: retrieval date.
  • Evidence tiers must be visible throughout (documented / interpretation / hypothesis).
  • AI assistance: structure and language only, with disclosure; the analysis must be yours and defensible orally. Undisclosed AI-generated analysis is a violation.

Grading: see final-key.md for model answers, point allocation, and the marker's rubric.
