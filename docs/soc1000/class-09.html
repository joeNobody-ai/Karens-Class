Session 9 — Gender and Sexuality

Big question. How is gender done — and what does a pageant show about it?

Where we start. A student runs for Miss Gilbert Hall the way another character did in 1988 — and around the pageant, the season's romance scripts are being rewritten in real time. Gender is not a thing you are; it is a thing you do, and a pageant is that doing on a public stage.

Before class: viewing

Text Episode First aired Why this one
Revival (2026) Ep 7, Sometimes You Get the Culture...Sometimes the Culture Gets You (second half) 2026-09-24 The Miss Gilbert Hall pageant; the romance scripts around it
Revival (2026) Ep 4, Those Who Can...Cuff 2026-09-24 "Cuffing season" and the changing rules of dating

Reference site for both, with sources: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/

Sociological frame

  1. Doing gender (West and Zimmerman): gender is not a fixed trait but an ongoing performance — something accomplished in interaction, held to a standard, and judged.
  2. Gender scripts: the shared expectations for how a man or a woman should behave in a given scene (a date, a pageant, a breakup). Scripts are learned, and they can be rewritten.
  3. Gender stratification: the unequal distribution of power and resources along gender lines — who is watched, who is judged, who does the invisible work.
  4. Intersectionality (intro): gender never operates alone; it is always shaped by race, class, and other positions at once.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–10 Screen the pageant. Ask: what is being performed, and what is being judged?
10–24 Define doing gender. Performance versus essence; the standard held up and the labour of meeting it
24–38 Gender scripts. The date, the pageant, the breakup as scripted scenes. Where the 2026 season rewrites the 1988 script, and where it does not
38–52 Gender stratification. Who is watched, who is judged, who does the invisible work of making the pageant happen
52–64 Pair drill. Each pair takes one scene and names the script, who enforces it, and where it breaks
64–75 Close and assign. Intersectionality, flagged to connect with Module 8. Assign homework 9

Discussion questions

  1. What is the difference between "being" a gender and "doing" one — in a pageant specifically?
  2. Name a gender script you follow without thinking. Where did you learn it, and who enforces it?
  3. What changed in the romance scripts between 1988 and 2026, and what stayed the same?
  4. Who does the invisible work in the scene — and who gets the visible credit?
  5. Why does gender never operate alone? Give an example where race or class changes how gender plays out.

Instructor notes

If a student says gender is "just biology." Separate the physical from the social: whatever biology supplies, the meaning of gender — the scripts, the stratification, the pageant — is social and varies across time and place. That variation is the evidence for construction.

If the room treats the pageant as trivial. It is one of the show's most sociological objects: a public competition in which gender, class, and colour are all on display and being scored at once. Take it seriously and the room will follow.

If a student raises colourism again. Good — this is where Module 8 and Module 9 meet: the pageant grades gender and skin tone in the same scorecard. That is intersectionality in one image.
