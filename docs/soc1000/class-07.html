Session 7 — Social Stratification and Class

Big question. Why do some students have more — and why does it matter?

Where we start. Hillman's money runs out. A student is already working to make rent while another never thinks about it. Stratification is the ranked arrangement of people into layers — and a campus, for all its talk of community, is one of the clearest places to see it.

Before class: viewing

Text Episode First aired Why this one
Revival (2026) Ep 9, Romancing the Unknown 2026-09-24 The budget crisis erupts; students organise; money pressure is everywhere
Original (1989) S2E41, Take This Job and Love It 1989-03-16 The same fight over money and work, 37 years earlier
Optional clip The moment the crisis goes public — An institution's stratification made explicit

Reference site for both, with sources: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/

Sociological frame

  1. Stratification: the systematic ranking of groups in a society, distributing resources and life chances unevenly. It is a structure, not a pile of individual differences.
  2. Social class: a position in that ranking, defined by wealth, income, education, occupation — and by the habits and expectations that come with it.
  3. Wealth versus income: income is what flows in; wealth is what you already have. The gap between them is the deepest part of American stratification and the least visible in a paycheck.
  4. Mobility: movement up or down the ladder — and the question of how much of it is actually open.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–10 Screen the crisis going public. Ask: who is calm and who is not, and what does their calm or panic tell you about their position?
10–24 Define stratification and class. Why stratification is structural, not personal; the difference between describing inequality and explaining it
24–38 Wealth versus income. Walk the distinction with a concrete example. Why two students can earn the same and face very different futures
38–52 Class inside the community. The show's own status competition (the bougie versus the scholarship student). Stratification does not stop at the gates of a Black institution
52–64 Pair drill. Each pair identifies one sign of class in the scene, one resource it tracks, and one thing it conceals
64–75 Close and assign. Flag the "culture versus structure" debate for Module 11. Assign homework 7

Discussion questions

  1. What is the difference between a student who is poor and a student who is broke for the month?
  2. How can two people earn the same amount and still be in different classes?
  3. Where do you see class at work inside a community that talks about itself as one family?
  4. Is mobility a matter of effort, structure, or both? What would you need to know to answer?
  5. What does a budget crisis reveal about an institution that a normal week conceals?

Instructor notes

If a student says "inequality is just how the world works." Separate description from explanation: describing that it works that way is not explaining why it works that way. Sociology exists for the second question.

If the room slides into blaming individuals. Keep the frame structural: a student's money problems are the same event as a college's budget crisis seen from two heights. The structure produces both.

If someone raises race and class together. Good; note they intersect but are not the same, and flag intersectionality for Module 8 and Module 9. Do not collapse one into the other.

stratification · social class · wealth versus income · mobility · inequality · life chances
