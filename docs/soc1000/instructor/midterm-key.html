Midterm key and grading rubric — INSTRUCTOR MATERIAL

Do not distribute to students. Model answers here are standards, not scripts: a strong student will take a different route and should be rewarded for it. What follows is what a full-credit answer must contain.

Part A1 (5 points) — The sociological imagination

Points What the answer must contain
2 The personal version stated from the inside (the student's own experience of the trouble).
2 The public version is genuinely sociological: it names a structural arrangement (cost of attendance and aid policy; or belonging and who a campus is built to include) rather than "society is unfair".
1 The "how would you test it" question is concrete and answerable (e.g. a comparison of completion rates by income; or a measure of belonging by background).

Common errors to mark down: restating the trouble twice with no structural move; or naming a structure without connecting it to the specific trouble.

Part A2 (5 points) — A documented fact and its interpretation

Points What the answer must contain
2 Documented: the two dates (24 September 1987; 24 September 2026) and the 39-year gap. Nothing more. Any claim that the pairing was intended is not documented.
2 Interpretation: a labelled reading (e.g. that launching on the same date stages the revival as an inheritance). Must be flagged as a reading, not stated as fact.
1 Unsettleable: whether the writers chose the date deliberately. Settling it would need a writer's statement or production document; saying the question stays open is correct, not weak.

Part B (10 points) — Concept application

Points Criterion Notes for the marker
3 Concept use Two concepts used correctly and for something (not named and dropped).
3 Documented fact At least one episode fact accurate (title + air date, or a published-summary detail).
2 Evidence discipline Documented facts and interpretations kept visibly apart.
2 Argument The answer makes a claim, not a summary; the claim answers the question asked.

B1 — Culture and ritual: what a full-credit answer contains

  • Ritual treated as shared practice generating collective feeling, not "a tradition".
  • One norm the ritual enforces (who belongs, how one participates), one boundary it draws (insider versus outsider), and one thing it conceals (the labour that produces it; the status competition dressed as school spirit; the cracking marriage behind the celebration).

B2 — Deviance and the label: what a full-credit answer contains

  • The 1991 episode's documented facts handled correctly: an HIV disclosure at the centre of a sitcom.
  • The label named, who applied it (the historical stigma around HIV), and the cost (spoiled identity, disclosure risk, social exclusion) — using Goffman's stigma.
  • Acknowledges the 2026 episode's crisis is not named in its published summary, if it is raised.

B3 — Stratification inside a community: what a full-credit answer contains

  • One concrete sign of class in Ep 9 (money pressure, the budget crisis, who is calm and who is not).
  • Wealth versus income distinguished correctly.
  • What the crisis reveals about the institution stated — the answer must do more than describe the plot.

Return-of-marks protocol

Return the midterm with one paragraph of feedback: one thing the answer did well, one thing the evidence handling needs, and whether the student's tier discipline is improving. Do not write grades on the front page and hand the pile back.
