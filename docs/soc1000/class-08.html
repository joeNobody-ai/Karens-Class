Session 8 — Race and Ethnicity

Big question. Is race something you are, or something you are assigned?

Where we start. The episode with the heaviest load: a talent show, an argument over African and diaspora identity, and a title that quotes 1987 back at itself. Race is real in its consequences and invented in its categories — and this episode puts that tension on stage.

Before class: viewing

Text Episode First aired Why this one
Revival (2026) Ep 7, Sometimes You Get the Culture...Sometimes the Culture Gets You (first half) 2026-09-24 The diaspora argument: who belongs to "Black," and who decides
Original (1990) S3E60, A World Alike 1990-02-15 An anti-apartheid fight inside a Black campus, 35 years earlier
Optional clip The talent show / diaspora exchange — Category contested from inside

Reference site for both, with sources: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/

Sociological frame

  1. Race as social construction: race is not a biological fact; it is a category people made — and keep making — with real, material consequences. "Socially constructed" does not mean "not real."
  2. Ethnicity: a shared cultural identity — language, ancestry, nation, religion — distinct from the racial category imposed on a group.
  3. Racial formation (Omi and Winant): the process by which racial categories are created, inhabited, and changed — never finished, always contested.
  4. Colourism: stratification within a racial group by skin tone — proof that the category itself has an internal hierarchy.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–10 Screen the diaspora exchange. Ask: who is "inside" the category in this scene, who is being put outside, and who is doing the putting?
10–24 Race versus ethnicity. Construction, not biology. Why "socially constructed" and "real" are the same sentence, not opposites
24–38 Racial formation. The category as a process — made, inhabited, contested. The episode's talent-show argument as category work in public
38–52 Colourism. Stratification inside the group; the pageant (the episode's second half, next week) as the clearest display. Bridge forward
52–64 Pair drill. Each pair names one claim in the scene about who "Black" includes, who makes the claim, and who is written out by it
64–75 Close and assign. Connect to Module 13 (divestment, movements). Assign homework 8

Discussion questions

  1. If race is socially constructed, why are its consequences so material?
  2. What is the difference between race and ethnicity in this episode?
  3. Who gets to define who belongs to "the Black experience" — and who is written out when it is defined tightly?
  4. Where have you seen colourism operate, inside a community you know?
  5. What does it mean to say a category is "never finished"?

Instructor notes

If a student says "race isn't real, so we should just stop using it." The consequences are real even where the biology is not — you cannot wish away a category that organizes housing, policing, and life chances. The question is not whether to stop using it but how to see it.

If the room is nervous about saying the word "race." Model the discipline: the category is the object of study, named in the third person, never performed, never asked of any student as a spokesperson. The vocabulary protocol in the instructor guide applies here in full.

If a student raises colourism personally. Do not make them the example. Move to the concept — colourism is documented in the literature and visible in the show — and thank them without extracting testimony.
