Midterm examination

SOC 1000 · Introduction to Sociology: Reading the Social World through A Different World Released: end of Module 7 · Due: start of Module 9 · Weight: 20% of the course grade Format: take-home · Total: 20 points

Instructions

Answer Part A and Part B. Submit as one document. Cite your sources; for anything online, include the retrieval date. You may use the course readings, the reference site, and your own notes.

The evidence rule applies. Keep these apart in every answer: documented facts (episode titles, air dates, credits, published summaries), interpretations (readings of what a scene argues), and your own hypotheses. Marks are lost for confusing them — not for holding either kind of view.

On AI assistance. Structure and language assistance is permitted with disclosure. The analysis and argument must be yours; you should be able to defend any sentence orally. Undisclosed AI-generated analysis is a violation.

Reference site: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/

Part A — Short answers (2 × 5 points = 10 points)

Answer both. 250 words each. A precise answer with a source beats a long one without.

A1. The sociological imagination

Using C. Wright Mills's distinction between personal troubles and public issues, take one of the following and restate it both ways: (a) a first-year student who cannot afford to stay enrolled; or (b) a student who feels they do not belong on their campus. Name the structural arrangement that makes the personal trouble a public issue, and say what question you would need to answer to test the public version.

A2. A documented fact and its interpretation

The original premiered on 24 September 1987; the revival premiered on 24 September 2026, 39 years later. State what is documented about this pairing, what is a defensible interpretation of it, and what cannot be settled with the evidence available. Then say why the distinction matters for how a sociologist writes about a text.

Part B — Concept application (10 points)

Answer one of the following. 400–500 words. Use two course concepts and one documented episode fact (title and air date, or a published summary detail).

B1. Culture and ritual

Using Ep 3 (Center de Stage, 2026-09-24), argue whether homecoming functions as a ritual in Durkheim's sense. Identify one norm the ritual enforces, one boundary it draws, and one thing it conceals.

B2. Deviance and the label

Using original S4E92 (If I Should Die Before I Wake, 1991-04-11) — the episode that put an HIV disclosure at the centre of a sitcom — explain what a label is, who applied it in that historical moment, and what the label cost the person it was applied to. Use the concept of stigma.

B3. Stratification inside a community

Using Ep 9 (Romancing the Unknown, 2026-09-24), describe one way social class is visible inside a community that calls itself one family. Distinguish wealth from income, and say what the budget crisis reveals about the institution that a normal week conceals.

Submission checklist

  • [ ] Both Part A answers, each with at least one source
  • [ ] One Part B answer, with two concepts and one documented fact named
  • [ ] Evidence tiers kept distinct and flagged
  • [ ] AI assistance disclosed if used
