Session 4 — Socialization and the Life Course

Big question. How does a person get made?

Where we start. The "aunties" walk into a club and a whole generation walks in behind them — women who played the same roles forty years ago, now the older generation looking at the younger one. Nobody is born knowing how to be a person; we are all taught, and the teachers are everywhere.

Text Episode First aired Why this one

Sociological frame

  1. Socialization: the lifelong process by which we learn the norms, values, and behaviours of our society. It is how "the social" gets inside a person.
  2. Agents of socialization: the people and institutions that do the teaching — family, peers, school, media. In this scene, the aunties are the agents.
  3. Anticipatory socialization: learning the norms of a role before you are in it — a student rehearsing adulthood, a first-year rehearsing belonging.
  4. Othermothering: the practice, named in Black feminist thought, of women mothering children who are not their own — a whole community sharing the work of raising the next generation.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–10 Screen the aunties' entrance. Ask: what is being taught, and by whom to whom?
10–24 Define socialization. Primary and secondary; agents of socialization; the life course as a series of roles we learn
24–38 The aunties as agents. Othermothering as a named practice; how a community does the work of raising its young; what the younger generation learns that no institution teaches
38–52 Anticipatory socialization. How students rehearse the selves they are becoming — the club, the romance, the adult identity they are trying on
52–64 Pair drill. Each pair names one thing the younger characters are learning in the scene, one agent teaching it, and one thing no one is teaching them
64–75 Close and assign. Resocialization and total institutions, flagged for Module 11. Assign homework 4

Discussion questions

  1. Who taught you the norms you follow most without thinking?
  2. What does the aunties' arrival teach the younger characters that a syllabus cannot?
  3. What is the difference between being socialized into a community and being accepted by it?
  4. Name a role you rehearsed before you actually held it. How did the rehearsal shape the performance?
  5. What happens to a group when its usual agents of socialization are absent?

Instructor notes

If the room thinks socialization is just childhood. The concept runs the whole life course: every new role — first-year, partner, worker, parent — is a fresh round of learning. The scene is adults socializing adults, which is the point.

If a student raises "acting white" or code-switching. Hold it: that is a live debate in the field (Fordham and Ogbu) and the course's language work touches it. Note it belongs to Module 8 and the lingo reference, and move on.

If someone asks whether the aunties are "a Black thing." Othermothering is named in Black feminist thought and documented in the literature, but extended kin care appears across many communities. Name both without flattening either.
