Sources

Two kinds of citation here. Books and articles are cited by author, title and year — no links, because links to paywalled literature rot fastest. The show and its record are cited through the reference project's documented pages, which carry their own sources and label every claim by tier.

Before you teach with any of these: links and statistics age quickly. Re-check every figure you put on a board before the term starts, and re-run the source check in the parent project.

Core text

  • OpenStax, Introduction to Sociology 3e (Rice University, 2021) — the course's core textbook. Free and open access (search "OpenStax introduction to sociology").
  • C. Wright Mills, The Sociological Imagination (1959), ch. 1 — the course's founding reading.

Concept sources (cited by author, title, year)

  • Émile Durkheim, The Elementary Forms of Religious Life (1912) — ritual and collective effervescence (Module 3).
  • Erving Goffman, The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life (1959) and Stigma (1963) — the self, stigma, and the label (Modules 6, 13).
  • Candace West and Don H. Zimmerman, "Doing Gender" (1987) — Module 9.
  • Michael Omi and Howard Winant, Racial Formation in the United States (3rd ed., 2014) — Module 8.
  • W. E. B. Du Bois, The Souls of Black Folk (1903), ch. 1 — the earliest statement of the perspective this course teaches.
  • Marcel Mauss, The Gift (1925) — the gift and reciprocity (Module 14).
  • David A. Snow and Robert D. Benford, "Ideology, Frame Resonance, and Participant Mobilization" (1988) — Module 13.
  • Geneva Smitherman, Black Talk (1994) and Rickford & Rickford, Spoken Soul (2000) — the language reference (see lingo.md).
