Session 10 — Family and Intimate Relationships

Big question. Is romance personal, or structural — or both?

Where we start. "Cuffing season" arrives and every relationship on campus gets messier at once. The title is a one-word edit of a 1987 episode — the rules underneath changed completely. Who we love, and how, is not just a matter of the heart; it is shaped by structure.

Before class: viewing

Text Episode First aired Why this one
Revival (2026) Ep 4, Those Who Can...Cuff 2026-09-24 The dating rules change; romance meets academic and money pressure
Original (1988) S2E26, Dream Lover 1988-11-03 The earlier version of the same script
Original (1990) S4E73, Whitley's Last Supper 1990-10-11 Family, class, and the question of who you marry
Optional clip The parents' marriage strain, Ep 3 and Ep 4 — A marriage as the family the younger generation watches

Reference site for both, with sources: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/

Sociological frame

  1. Family forms: family is not one thing — nuclear, extended, chosen, and everything between. What counts as family is a social question, not a biological one.
  2. Kinship: the web of relations by blood, marriage, and choice — and the obligations that come with them. The aunties are kin whether or not they are relatives.
  3. Mate selection and the marriage market: who pairs with whom is patterned — by class, race, education, and geography. The "market" is a structure wearing the mask of a feeling.
  4. Othermothering (from Module 4, returning): family as something a community does, not just something it has.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–10 Screen the cuffing-season setup. Ask: what is being chosen freely here, and what is being chosen for the characters?
10–24 Define family forms and kinship. Chosen versus given; the aunties as kin; what "family" does that a roommate does not
24–38 Mate selection as structure. Who is "available" to whom, and why. The marriage market; the title edit as evidence the rules changed
38–52 The parents' marriage. The older generation's relationship as the structure the younger one watches — and partly inherits
52–64 Pair drill. Each pair names one relationship in the week's material and separates what is personal in it from what is structural
64–75 Close and assign. Bridge to Module 11 (the institution the family and the college both are). Assign homework 10

Discussion questions

  1. What is chosen in a relationship, and what is chosen for you before you even meet?
  2. Is the aunties' bond a family? Argue from the definition, not the feeling.
  3. What does the title edit — "Those Who Can...Cuff" — tell you about what changed between 1988 and 2026?
  4. How does a parent's marriage shape a child's romance, without anyone saying a word about it?
  5. Why is "the marriage market" a structure rather than just a metaphor?

Instructor notes

If the room thinks love is purely personal. Concede the feeling and hold the pattern: individual people fall in love individually, but who is available to whom — by class, race, geography, education — is patterned before any two of them meet. The personal and the structural are both true, which is the course's whole point.

If a student raises the family "culture" debate (the Moynihan question). Flag it: that is the loaded and live argument about whether Black family outcomes are explained by culture or by structure, and it is Module 11's material. Do not resolve it here.

If someone asks whether "cuffing season" is real. It is documented as the episode's own premise (the summary says "cuffing season arrives"), and the word itself is in the language reference. The sociological question is not whether it is real but what it does — it names a seasonal structure for romance.
