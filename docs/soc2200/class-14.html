Module 14 — Racial Identity and Belonging

Big question. Who decides who is Black — and what is at stake in the answer?

Where we start. We now screen Ep 7 whole — the talent show, the diaspora argument, the pageant — and read it as a single question: who gets to decide who belongs, and what follows from the answer. A World Alike (1990) asked the same question a generation earlier, when the boundaries of Blackness were being redrawn in public. This is the module that pulls identity itself onto the table.

Before class: viewing

Original (1990) S3E60, A World Alike 1990-02-15 The throwback that first put the boundaries of Blackness on screen.

Sociological frame

  1. Identity: the story people tell about who they are, assembled from available categories — never simply chosen, never simply imposed.
  2. Belonging: the social fact of being recognised as a member, with the access and obligation that recognition carries.
  3. Category work: the ongoing labour of sorting people into categories, policing the boundaries, and negotiating who fits.
  4. The boundaries of Blackness: the contested edges of a category that is real in its consequences but not fixed in its membership — who is inside, who is outside, and who rules on it.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–8 Settle and screen. Ep 7 in full, with one question in hand: who gets to rule on belonging?
8–22 Identity is chosen and imposed. Lecture the double movement — you assemble yourself from categories you did not choose.
22–36 Category work. Introduce the labour of sorting and policing boundaries, and find it inside the talent show and the pageant.
36–50 Who rules? Small groups: in the scene, who holds the authority to say who is "really" Black — and what gives them that authority?
50–63 Across the generations. Set A World Alike (1990) beside 2026 and ask what has moved in the boundaries of Blackness.
63–75 Close and assign. Restate the big question; assign Homework 14 (a ruled-on boundary, analysed).

Discussion questions

  1. Who in the scene has the authority to decide who is Black — and where does that authority come from?
  2. Is belonging something you earn, something you claim, or something you are granted? What does the scene show?
  3. What is at stake in being ruled in or out of the category? Name the concrete consequence.
  4. How does category work make a boundary feel natural when it is actually maintained by people?
  5. What moved between 1990 and 2026 in how Blackness is bounded — and what did not?

Instructor notes

If a student asks "so who is Black?" Refuse the essentialist bait gently: the sociological question is not the fact of the category but who is authorised to rule on it and with what consequences. Turn it back to the scene.

If the room becomes a tribunal on a real person's authenticity. Interrupt and re-ground: the course analyses categories, not individuals in the room or in the news.
