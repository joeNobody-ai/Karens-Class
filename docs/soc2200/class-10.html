Module 10 — Race and Education: The HBCU

Big question. What does a historically Black college do that a generic campus cannot?

Where we start. We revisit Ep 9, now read as a story about an institution: Hillman is not just a setting but a historically Black college, and the budget crisis is a question about what such an institution is for and who it serves. Take This Job and Love It (1989) takes us inside the working life of the institution itself.

Before class: viewing

Text Episode First aired Why this one
Revival (2026) Ep 9, Romancing the Unknown (revisit — the institution) 2026 The budget crisis, re-screened, now read as a fight over what a historically Black college is for.

Sociological frame

  1. HBCUs: historically Black colleges and universities — institutions founded to educate Black Americans when mainstream higher education would not, and which still do distinct work today.
  2. Education: a formal system that sorts and credentials people, and that has historically been a primary site of racial inclusion and exclusion.
  3. The color of schooling: the way schooling is organised and funded along racial lines, from segregation to present-day resource gaps.
  4. Institutions: durable patterns of social organisation — with rules, roles, and resources — that outlast any single person and shape behaviour regardless of individual intent.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–8 Settle and re-screen. The Ep 9 fight over the college's future, now with the question: what is this institution for?
8–22 Why HBCUs exist. Lecture the founding logic: built to do what a segregated system refused to do.
22–36 Institutions outlast people. Define institutions and show that Hillman's value is not any one person but a durable arrangement.
36–50 The color of schooling. Trace how schooling is racialised — in who gets in, who is funded, and what is taught.
50–63 What the crisis threatens. Small groups: if Hillman closes or shrinks, what exactly is lost that a generic campus cannot replace?
63–75 Close and assign. Restate the big question; assign Homework 10 (compare one institution's function).

Discussion questions

  1. What does Hillman do for its students that a majority-white campus cannot or will not?
  2. If an institution outlasts any person, what is actually at stake when it is threatened?
  3. How is schooling "colored" even where the law is colour-blind?
  4. In the scene, are students defending a building, a budget, or a social function? Which, and how do you know?
  5. What would count as evidence that an HBCU's work could be done just as well elsewhere?

Instructor notes

If a student asks "why do we still need HBCUs?" Treat it as a live sociological question, not a provocation: ask them to name the function the institution serves and whether that function persists.

If the conversation drifts into institutional pride or nostalgia. Redirect to mechanism: what specifically does the institution do, and for whom, that a generic campus does not?
