Module 2 — Race, Ethnicity, and Racial Formation

Big question. How is a racial category made — and remade — over time?

Where we start. A category does not appear fully formed; it is assembled, argued over, and reassembled. In the first half of Ep 7 the end-of-year talent show becomes the site of an argument about African versus diaspora identity, and we watch a category — who counts as authentically Black — get made in real time. The 1990 original A World Alike made the same move three decades earlier.

Before class: viewing

Sociological frame

  1. Ethnicity: shared ancestry, culture, or descent that people use to make sense of themselves and sort each other — voluntary in principle, often compulsory in practice.
  2. Racial formation (Omi and Winant, 2014): the sociohistorical process by which racial categories are created, inhabited, transformed, and destroyed.
  3. Omi and Winant: the sociologists who argued that race is not a fixed essence but a changing outcome of social forces and political conflict.
  4. The racial project: a specific interpretation, representation, or policy that links what a category means to how it is organised in institutions.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–8 Settle and screen. The first half of Ep 7: the talent show and the identity argument. Ask for facts first.
8–22 Race vs. ethnicity. Lecture the distinction, then ask which of the two is doing the work in the argument on stage.
22–36 Racial formation. Introduce Omi and Winant's process — a category made, contested, and remade — and map the stage argument onto it.
36–50 The racial project. Small groups each name a project (a word, a costume, a rule) inside the scene and say what meaning it links to what arrangement.
50–63 Across the eras. Place A World Alike (1990) beside the 2026 scene: what changed in the argument, what did not.
63–75 Close and assign. Restate the big question; assign Homework 2 (build your own racial project).

Discussion questions

  1. In the argument on stage, is the contested category race or ethnicity? Defend the line you draw.
  2. What does it take for a category to feel "natural" rather than made? Name a mechanism the scene shows.
  3. How does a racial project turn an interpretation into an arrangement?
  4. What would Omi and Winant say the talent show is doing, sociologically?
  5. If the category is always being remade, what keeps it from being remade away entirely?

Instructor notes

If a student says "ethnicity is just culture, race is biology." That is the essentialist slip the whole course exists to undo. Ask them to restate it as a constructionist claim using the scene, and note that Omi and Winant treat both as socially produced.

If the diaspora-vs.-African debate gets personal in the room. Invoke norm 1 (no spokespeople) and turn the question back to the text: "what does the scene show us about who gets to define authenticity?"
