SOC 2200 — Race and Ethnicity in American Society — Final

Released/due: final session (Module 15) · Weight: 30% of the final grade · Format: take-home, open-book · Total: 100 points

Instructions

Answer every question. This is open-book: you may consult the modules, the readings, and the reference site (https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/).

Two rules govern everything you write:

  1. The evidence rule. Keep three kinds of claim apart: documented (an episode title, air date, credit, or published summary), interpretation (your reading, labelled as such), and hypothesis (a testable claim). Do not invent dialogue, quotes, plot beats, or statistics. Where you rely on a summary, say "the published summary states."
  2. AI note. You may use AI to check grammar, but every claim you submit must be yours and sourced. A fabricated or hallucinated source is an academic-integrity violation however it was produced.

Part A — Short answers (5 × 12 points = 60)

Answer each in a short paragraph. Define the concept, then apply it to the documented episode fact named in the question.

A1. Define racial formation (Omi and Winant). The published summary of Ep 10, A Gift & The Curse, states that major decisions are made about the college's future. Explain how a decision about an institution is also a moment of racial formation — a category being made, inhabited, or remade.

A2. Define intersectionality (Crenshaw). The published summary of Ep 7 states that Hazel runs for Miss Gilbert Hall and that the students deal with sexual health and relationships. Show why a single-axis analysis (race only, or gender only) fails to capture what that summary points to.

A3. Define the racial wealth gap (Oliver and Shapiro) and explain the difference between wealth and income. The published summary of Ep 9, Romancing the Unknown, states that Hillman's budget crisis erupts. Which of the two — wealth or income — does a budget crisis most directly threaten, and why does the distinction matter?

A4. Define mass incarceration (Alexander) and criminalization. The published summary of Honeymoon in L.A. (part 1) (S6E120, first aired 1992-09-24) places a honeymoon against the 1992 Los Angeles uprising. Explain what a family sitcom does, sociologically, by carrying that backdrop on screen.

A5. Define the boundaries of Blackness as a form of category work. The published summary of Ep 7 states that Kojo is caught up in arguments over African and diaspora identity. Who, in that argument, holds the authority to rule on belonging — and what is at stake in the ruling?

Part B — Applied essay (40 points)

Choose one of the following. Your essay must (a) state the documented facts you use and name their source; (b) deploy at least three concepts from anywhere in the course, each doing distinct work; (c) make an arguable claim and defend it; and (d) name one honest limit of what the case can show.

  1. Analyse the season finale A Gift & The Curse (Ep 10) as a case of racial formation: hold the crisis, the family situation, and the decision about the college's future against the course's concepts.
  2. Set a 2026 revival scene against its 1987–1993 original (for example, A World Alike, S3E60, 1990-02-15, against the Ep 7 diaspora argument), and argue what changed and what did not in how the two eras make race.
