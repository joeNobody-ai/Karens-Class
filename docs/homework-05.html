Homework 5 — Verify before you argue

Due: start of Session 6 · Length: 1.5 pages · Weight: counts in the homework average

Task

This is a data-literacy exercise. You will find one number yourself, from a primary source, and then use it carefully.

  1. Find one current, citable statistic about Black Americans' health in an area relevant to Sessions 5 or 7 — HIV, sexual health, maternal health, or access to care. Use a primary source: a national public health agency, a national statistical agency, or a peer-reviewed study. Not a news article about a study, and not a summary site.
  2. Report it in this exact format: - Figure: - Source (agency, report or table name, year): - URL: - Date you retrieved it: - Denominator / comparison group: - One thing this figure cannot tell us:
  3. Then use it. In 250 words, make an argument about the revival's or the original's depiction of a health crisis, using your figure. The argument must be honest about what the number does and does not support.
  4. One sentence on the 1991 episode. Why did a Black sitcom's disclosure episode matter more than the same information in a public health pamphlet?

Sources

  • Original: S4E92 If I Should Die Before I Wake (1991-04-11)
  • Revival: Ep 5 Final Words — watch before class if you are able; the summaries do not name the crisis
  • The callback cards behind this session, including the crisis slot and the 1991 episode: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/ep05.html#ep05-quiet-crisis-slot and the sexual-health card at https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/ep07.html#ep07-sexual-health
  • Primary-source starting points (all HTTP-checked for the course build; verify they still resolve): the CDC's National Center for Health Statistics (https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/index.html) and CDC HIV statistics pages; KFF's racial equity and health policy collection (https://www.kff.org/racial-equity-and-health-policy/)
  • Cathy J. Cohen, The Boundaries of Blackness (1999), introduction — for the politics of who counted

Criteria (out of 10)

Points For
3 The source is primary, current, and correctly described
3 Denominator and comparison group are stated (the most common failure in public argument)
2 The 250-word argument does not overclaim what the figure supports
2 The final sentence explains what television could do that a pamphlet could not

If you cannot find a figure you trust: say so, name what you tried, and explain what the absence itself might indicate about how this health issue is measured. Do not invent a number. Do not cite a number you have not opened.
