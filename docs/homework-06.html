Homework 6 — Who carries the memory?

Due: start of Session 7 · Length: 1.5 pages · Weight: counts in the homework average

Task

  1. Build a memory table. Choose five characters who appear in the 2026 revival and also existed in the original series (check the cast bridge at https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/#cast). For each: - the character's name in both eras, - one specific documented original event they carry (season, episode, air date, what happened), - how the revival uses that memory — explained, implied, or ignored.
  2. The unexplained memory. Pick the one the revival leans on hardest without explaining it. Write 150 words on what a viewer who has not seen the original would miss, and whether the episode still works for them.
  3. The "aunties" as an institution. In 2026 Episode 6, the older women arrive and take over the room. Describe the mechanism by which they hold authority — not "they are respected", but what actually makes the scene work that way.
  4. One sentence. Rank the five characters by how much the revival's story depends on them, and justify the top choice.

Sources

  • Cast bridge and the aunties' callback cards: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/ep06.html and https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/#cast
  • Original: S2E23 Dr. War Is Hell (1988-10-06) — the 1988 episode in which several of these characters enter the series
  • Patricia Hill Collins, Black Feminist Thought (1990), ch. 6 — othermothering
  • Maurice Halbwachs, On Collective Memory — for the idea that groups remember through members

Criteria (out of 10)

Points For
4 Table entries are documented (season, episode, date) rather than impressionistic
2 The 150 words distinguish missing information from missing meaning
3 The authority mechanism is described as a mechanism (who grants it, what enforces it, what it costs)
1 The ranking is argued, not asserted

Reminder: if you have not seen the original episodes, say which claims are based on the documented summaries and which are inferences. Both are acceptable; confusing them is not.
