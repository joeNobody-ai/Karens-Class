Homework 13 — Steel-man and falsify

Due: start of Session 14 · Length: 2 pages · Weight: counts in the homework average

Task

This is the discipline's hardest intellectual move: state your opponent's case better than they did, then decide what evidence would settle the matter.

  1. Choose one family or intimacy plot from either era — for example, a character's relationship with a parent, a couple's conflict over money, or the show's treatment of a single parent. Describe it in 150 words.
  2. Steel-man the culture explanation. In 300 words, argue that the behaviour in this plot is explained by culture — transmitted values, norms, expectations. Make it as strong as you can. Cite at least one real source that makes a version of this argument.
  3. Steel-man the structural explanation. In 300 words, argue that it is explained by structure — money, jobs, housing, schooling, policing, credit. Cite at least one real source.
  4. Falsification. For each explanation, state what evidence would show it is wrong. Be specific: what would you need to observe, measure, or read?
  5. Verdict. In 150 words, say which explanation the text supports, and — this is the graded part — say whether the text could settle the question at all. A television drama about six people cannot demonstrate a population-level mechanism. What can it do instead?

Sources

  • Daniel Patrick Moynihan, The Negro Family: The Case for National Action (1965)
  • Herbert G. Gutman, The Black Family in Slavery and Freedom, 1750–1925 (1976), introduction
  • William Julius Wilson, The Truly Disadvantaged (1987), ch. 1
  • Annette Lareau, Unequal Childhoods (2003), introduction
  • Prudence L. Carter, Keepin' It Real (2005)
  • Mary Pattillo, Black Picket Fences (1999)
  • The episodes: 2026 Ep 4 and Ep 6; original S4E73 Whitley's Last Supper (1990-10-11)
  • The callback cards for the plots you analyse, for documented episode facts: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/ep04.html and https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/ep06.html

Criteria (out of 10)

Points For
4 Both explanations are steel-manned and sourced — no straw men
3 Falsification conditions are concrete and obtainable
2 The verdict distinguishes what a text can and cannot demonstrate
1 Sources are cited accurately (author, year, argument)

The trap: most students steel-man the explanation they already agree with. If your culture paragraph is weaker than your structure paragraph, you have not done the assignment — you have restated your view twice.
