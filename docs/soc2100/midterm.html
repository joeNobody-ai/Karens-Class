SOC 2100 — Social Problems · Midterm Examination

Released: after Module 7 · Due: before Module 9 · Weight: 20% of the course grade Format: take-home, open-book, open-note · Total: 100 points · Scope: Modules 1–7

Instructions

  • Answer Part A and Part B. Part A questions are each worth 10 points; Part B is worth 40 points.
  • The evidence rule applies throughout. Mark documented facts (an episode title, air date, credit, or published summary) as documented, and your readings of a scene as your interpretation. Do not invent dialogue, quotes, or statistics. If you are unsure whether a detail is documented, say what the published summary states and stop there.
  • AI note. You may use generative tools to brainstorm or check grammar and must disclose any such use. You may not use them to generate a claim, an analysis, a figure, or a citation you cannot stand behind from a primary source. A fabricated statistic or episode fact is a failing answer.
  • Cite the episodes by title (and, for originals, air date). You may use the reference site: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/

Part A — Short answers (6 × 10 = 60 points)

Answer each in a paragraph, defining the concept and applying it to the named, documented episode fact.

  1. Claims-making. Define claims-making (Best) and apply it to the difference between the 2026 title Irreconcilable Differences and the original title Reconcilable Differences (S1E1, 1987-09-24). What does the difference in titles claim about the same family situation?

  2. Wealth versus income. Define wealth and income and explain why the wealth gap is much larger than the income gap. Then connect your answer to the budget crisis shown in Romancing the Unknown (2026, Ep 9): is the crisis a story about a flow or a stock?

  3. Racial formation. Define racial formation (Omi & Winant) and apply it to the diaspora argument in the first half of Sometimes You Get the Culture...Sometimes the Culture Gets You (2026, Ep 7). What category is being made or contested, and by whom?

  4. Doing gender. Define doing gender (West & Zimmerman) and apply it to the Miss Gilbert Hall contest in the second half of Ep 7, contrasting it with Mr. Hillman (S1E17, 1988-02-25). What does the reversal of the contest make visible?

  5. Stigma. Define stigma (Goffman) and explain why the published summary of Final Words (2026, Ep 5) refusing to name the crisis matters sociologically. What is the summary declining to do?

  6. Credentialing. Define credentialing (Collins) and apply it to the work-versus-school tradeoff in Take This Job and Love It (S2E41, 1989-03-16). What does a degree cost a working student, and what does it buy?

Part B — Applied question (40 points)

Choose one 2026 scene from Modules 1–7 and one original episode from the list below, and answer:

Is the social problem these scenes show better explained by structure or by individual agency? Defend your answer.

Your essay must: (a) define structure and agency; (b) state at least one documented fact about each text you use; (c) mark your interpretations as interpretations; (d) argue one side, engaging the strongest version of the other; and (e) name one thing your chosen account cannot explain.

Originals you may use: Reconcilable Differences (S1E1, 1987-09-24); Mr. Hillman (S1E17, 1988-02-25); Dream Lover (S2E26, 1988-11-03); Take This Job and Love It (S2E41, 1989-03-16); A World Alike (S3E60, 1990-02-15); Whitley's Last Supper (S4E73, 1990-10-11); If I Should Die Before I Wake (S4E92, 1991-04-11).
