Module 8 — Crime, Deviance, and the Justice System

Big question. Who gets labelled a criminal, and what does the label do?

Where we start. Deborah speaks her mind in public and is called out — an act that could read as free speech or as a transgression, depending on who is naming it. We use that hinge to study how deviance is a label applied by audiences, and how the label itself changes what happens next.

Before class: viewing

Text Episode First aired Why this one
Revival (2026) Ep 2, O Captain! My Captain! 2026 A public "call-out" puts the labeling process — rule, transgression, response — on screen
Original (1992) S6E120, Honeymoon in L.A. (part 1) 1992-09-24 A throwback set in a city — the context where policing and labeling are most visible
Optional clip The Rodney King precedent card (ep09-rodney-king-precedent) — To connect the show's era to a documented moment in criminalization

Sociological frame

  1. Deviance (Becker 1963): behavior that people so label — deviance is created by the social response, not by the act itself.
  2. Labeling theory (Becker 1963): the account of how a label, once applied, sticks and reshapes a person's identity and opportunities.
  3. Criminalization: the process by which a behavior or a population is defined as criminal and brought under the justice system's authority.
  4. Policing (OpenStax 2021): the organized enforcement of law — whose discretion about whom to stop, cite, and arrest is a central sociological fact.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–8 Opening poll. "What makes an act a crime?" — collect answers; sort into "the act itself" and "the response to it."
8–22 Deviance as a response. Becker defined; the class re-reads its own poll as evidence for labeling.
22–36 The call-out. Read the published summary of O Captain! My Captain!: what is the "rule," who is said to break it, and who is doing the naming?
36–50 The label's consequences. Small groups trace one act through three stages: act → label → changed life chances.
50–63 Criminalization and discretion. Using the Rodney King precedent card and Honeymoon in L.A. (part 1) (1992-09-24), ask whose behavior gets policed and whose does not.
63–75 Close and assign. Assign Homework 8 (a labeling-trace); confirm the midterm is due before Module 9.

Discussion questions

  1. Is deviance in the act or in the audience? What does Becker's answer commit you to?
  2. Who is doing the "calling out" in O Captain! My Captain!, and what power do they hold?
  3. Once a label sticks, what does it change about a person's path — independent of anything they did?
  4. Why does discretion in policing matter more than the letter of the law for explaining who is labelled?
  5. What would a labeling account say about "crime rates" that a "criminal behavior" account would not?

Instructor notes

If the room collapses "crime" into "bad people." Hold the distinction: the course asks who gets labelled, not who is bad. Return to the act/response sorting from the opening poll.

If discussion of policing becomes polarized. Anchor it to the documented cards — the precedent card and the 1992 episode — and keep the question on mechanism (discretion, labeling), not verdicts about individuals.
