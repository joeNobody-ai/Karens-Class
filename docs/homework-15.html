Homework 15 — Capstone memo (public-facing analysis)

Due: start of Session 15, presented in class · Length: 1,200 words (±10%) · Weight: 10% of the course grade

Task

Write for an educated reader who has not taken this course and may not have watched either show. The memo must make the course's central claim concrete: that Black experience, as this 39-year case shows it, is constitutive of American society — not a specialty topic within it.

Required structure

  1. A hook (100 words). One scene, one moment, one documented fact. No throat-clearing.
  2. The claim (150 words). Your version of the course's argument, stated so that a sceptical reader could disagree with it. If it cannot be disagreed with, it is not a claim.
  3. The evidence (600 words). Two or three paired moments — one from 2026, one from 1987–1993 — analysed with at least three course concepts. Each pair must do analytical work: what does the comparison show that a single text could not?
  4. The honesty section (150 words). Explicitly account for your evidence: which claims are documented, which are your interpretation, and what you could not establish. Name one limitation of the two shows as evidence about Black life.
  5. The stakes (200 words). What follows if your claim is right — for how sociology is taught, for how the media is read, or for both. No platitudes.

Citations. At least four sources: two from the course reading, one primary source (data, an episode's documented facts, an archival item), and one of your own choosing that the course did not assign. Use a consistent citation style and include the retrieval date for anything online.

Sources

  • The reference site, for documented episode facts and the callback pairs: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/
  • The course bibliography: ../sources.md
  • Any of the course's required books and articles

Rubric (also in ../rubrics.md, out of 20 points)

Points Criterion
4 Claim — arguable, specific, and clearly yours
5 Evidence — pairs are well chosen and the analysis is genuinely comparative
4 Concept use — at least three course concepts, used correctly and not merely named
3 Honesty — evidence tiers handled explicitly; limitations named
2 Stakes — consequences argued rather than asserted
2 Prose — clear for a non-specialist; correct citation; no padding

Presentation (ungraded but required): 90 seconds. State your claim, your best piece of evidence, and the one thing you could not settle. The class will hunt for your unexamined assumption.

AI policy reminder: assistance with structure or language is permitted with disclosure. The claim, the analysis, and the honesty section must be yours, and you should be able to defend any sentence orally.
