Homework 4 — Vocabulary as evidence

Due: start of Session 5 · Length: 1.5 pages · Weight: counts in the homework average

Task

Language is data. Collect the words the two shows use for the same activities and analyse what the vocabulary assumes.

  1. Build a table with two columns: 1987–1993 terms and 2026 terms, for the general area of romantic and sexual relationships. Aim for at least six terms per column. Use the episode summaries and callback cards for the revival, and the original episode list for the earlier era. Where a term is your own gloss rather than a word in the text, mark it.
  2. For each pair (or nearest equivalent), state what the word assumes about: duration, exclusivity, formality, and who is expected to initiate.
  3. One paragraph. What does "cuffing season" imply that "going together" does not — and what does that implication tell you about the conditions under which people form relationships now?
  4. One counter-example. Find a term in either era that does not fit your pattern, and say what it does to your analysis. A pattern that survives its counter-example is worth having.

Sources

  • Ep 4 callback cards, including the title echo and the romance chain: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/ep04.html
  • Original: S2E26 Dream Lover (1988-11-03); S1E4 Those Who Can't...Tutor (1987-10-22)
  • Optional: Candace West and Don H. Zimmerman, "Doing Gender" (1987) — for the idea that gender is produced in interaction and accountable to an audience

Criteria (out of 10)

Points For
4 At least 12 terms, correctly attributed to an era, with glosses marked as yours where they are
3 The four assumptions are applied consistently to each pair
2 The paragraph makes an argument about conditions, not just about slang
1 The counter-example is real and honestly handled

Trap: do not assume the older vocabulary was more moral and the newer one more casual. The evidence is the words, and what the words make possible.
