Homework 2 — One conflict, three frames

Due: start of Session 3 · Length: 1.5 pages · Weight: counts in the homework average

Task

Take the conflict in 2026 Episode 2 (O Captain! My Captain!), in which a student is publicly called out after expressing an opinion. Then:

  1. Three frames, 150 words each. Write the same events three times: - from the position of the student who spoke, - from the position of whoever called her out, - from the position of a third party with something to lose (a donor, a coach, an administrator with a budget). Each version must be internally consistent — you are not writing parody, you are writing the frame.
  2. Cost. For each frame, state who bears the cost and what the cost is. Use a table.
  3. The 1989 comparison. In one paragraph, put your analysis next to Radio Free Hillman (S2E32): the dean refuses to punish the sit-in and suspends the student radio host instead. Is that the same cost-allocation problem with a different answer, or a different problem?
  4. One sentence. Which frame does the revival's episode itself adopt? Defend it with one scene.

Sources

  • Ep 2 callback cards, including the 1989 origin: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/ep02.html
  • Original: S2E32 Radio Free Hillman (1989-01-05)
  • Optional: Doug McAdam, Political Process and the Development of Black Insurgency (1982), ch. 2

Criteria (out of 10)

Points For
4 All three frames are internally consistent and would be defensible by someone who held them
3 Cost allocation is stated precisely — who pays, in what currency (money, standing, time, safety)
2 The 1989 comparison identifies a real difference or argues convincingly for sameness
1 The final sentence is a claim that could be wrong

Common failure: writing the second and third frames as straw men. If a frame is obviously idiotic, you have not written the frame yet — you have written the joke about it.
