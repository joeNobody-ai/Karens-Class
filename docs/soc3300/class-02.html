Module 2 — Ritual and Collective Effervescence

Big question. What does a group feel when it does something together — and why does it matter?

Where we start. Module 1 asked what culture is; this module asks what it does to a group. We return to the homecoming (the 2026 Center de Stage and its 1988 throwback, A Stepping Stone, 1988-12-01) and read it through Émile Durkheim's account of ritual: the claim that when people assemble around a shared symbol and do the same thing at the same time, they generate a heightened, self-reinforcing collective feeling — effervescence — out of which the group's sense of itself is made. The published summary of Center de Stage gives us the assembly (a headlining performance, the return of Dwayne and Whitley) and the original gives us the ritual form (the step).

Sociological frame

  1. Durkheim (Émile Durkheim, The Elementary Forms of the Religious Life, 1912): society is held together not only by interests but by shared representations, and these are generated in assembly.
  2. Ritual: a standardized, repeated, symbol-laden sequence of action that a group performs together — the step, the homecoming assembly — whose meaning lies in the doing, not in any message stated.
  3. Collective effervescence: the heightened emotional state a group reaches when it acts in unison, which Durkheim argued is the crucible in which sacred symbols and group identity are forged.
  4. Solidarity: the felt bond of membership the ritual produces — mechanical (sameness) or organic (interdependence) — which outlasts the ritual moment and can be called back later.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–8 Re-enter the homecoming. Recall the documented facts from Module 1; state Durkheim's question: not "what does it mean?" but "what does the group feel, and what does that feeling make?"
8–22 Durkheim, unpacked. Lay out ritual → effervescence → solidarity. Distinguish Durkheim's causal story from a mere "mood"; ask what evidence would show the chain at work.
22–36 The step as ritual. Using the reference card, identify the ritual features — repetition, synchronization, shared symbol, audience as participant. Ask what the step does that a speech cannot.
36–50 Who re-ignites the feeling. The reference card documents the arrival of the "aunties" (original cast). Ask what their presence does to the assembly's emotional intensity, and why a return electrifies.
50–63 Effervescence and its costs. The published summary states that Dwayne and Whitley return with visible tensions. Ask whether ritual can overwrite strain, or whether strain shows through — and what Durkheim would predict.
63–75 Close and assign. Name the mechanism (assembly → feeling → solidarity); assign Homework 2; preview Module 3's turn from what ritual does to who makes the culture that gets ritualized.

Discussion questions

  1. Durkheim argued that effervescence is generated by assembly, not merely released by it. What evidence from the two scenes would support or challenge that?
  2. Is a step routine a ritual in Durkheim's sense, or just a coordinated performance? What is the difference that matters?
  3. The published summary states the marriage is strained. Can a group feel effervescent while its central figures are in conflict? What does your answer imply about solidarity?
  4. The "aunties" are documented as original-cast arrivals. Why does the return of past participants intensify collective feeling — and what theory from Module 1 helps explain it?
  5. If ritual makes solidarity, what happens to a group that stops assembling? Can the solidarity be stored, or does it decay?

Instructor notes

If the room treats effervescence as mere "hype." Re-anchor: Durkheim's claim is that the feeling produces the sacred and the group's self-image — it is causal, not decorative. Ask what would be lost if the step were performed by one person alone.

If a student claims to know what the cast "really felt." That is neither documented nor a reading; it is projection. Redirect to what the text and the summary show — the assembly, the return, the tensions — and ask what a Durkheimian would predict from those facts.
