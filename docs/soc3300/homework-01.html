Homework 1 — Culture has, culture does

Due: start of Module 2 · Length: 1 page · Weight: counts in the homework average (lowest two dropped)

Task

  1. Choose one recurring cultural practice from your own life (a family routine, a team or club ritual, a way your household does a holiday). Do not use television or media for this one — use something you do.
  2. Describe it twice, in two paragraphs. First in Geertz's terms: as a "web of meaning" the practice spins and that you inhabit. Then in Swidler's terms: as a "tool" the practice is — what problem it solves, what strategy of action it serves.
  3. In one closing paragraph, argue which description better captures your practice, and state what the losing description misses. This is the whole point: the two theories are not both "right," they reveal different things.

Sources

  • Clifford Geertz, The Interpretation of Cultures (1973) — the web-of-meaning view
  • Ann Swidler, "Culture in Action: Symbols and Strategies" (1986) — the toolkit view
  • The reference site, for how the course reads a real case: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/

Criteria (out of 10)

Points For
4 The two descriptions are genuinely different — Geertz's and Swidler's are used as rival lenses, not synonyms
3 The closing argument takes a side and names what the other lens misses
2 The practice is concrete and specific (a reader could picture it)
1 Evidence discipline: your own observation is marked as yours, nothing borrowed is presented as fact
