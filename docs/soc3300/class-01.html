Module 1 — What Is Culture?

Big question. Is culture what a group has, or what a group does?

Where we start. Every seminar in the sociology of culture has to begin by refusing the everyday meaning of the word — "culture" as the opera, the museum, the things a group has. We open instead with a working definition of culture as what a group does to make the world mean something, and we test it against one documented case: the homecoming in the 2026 revival episode Center de Stage, where the published summary states that Deborah tries to secure a headlining performer while Dwayne and Whitley return with visible tensions in their marriage, and its 1988 throwback, A Stepping Stone (1988-12-01).

Sociological frame

  1. Culture (Raymond Williams; Clifford Geertz): a "whole way of life" — the shared practices, symbols, and meanings a group uses to interpret its situation, not merely the arts or refined taste.
  2. The toolkit (Ann Swidler, 1986): culture as a repertoire of strategies, habits, and symbols that actors draw on to solve problems — what people use rather than what they have.
  3. Meaning-making (Max Weber; Geertz): the human activity of attaching significance to action; sociology reads conduct as meaningful to its participants before it reads it as caused.
  4. Swidler versus Geertz: whether culture is best modeled as a coherent web of meaning that people inhabit (Geertz) or a loose toolkit of resources people deploy in "strategies of action" (Swidler) — the course's founding argument, to be returned to all term.

The 75 minutes

Min What
0–8 What is "culture," really? Collect the room's everyday definitions; sort them into "culture as possession" and "culture as activity." State the big question.
8–22 Two theories, one case. Introduce Geertz's web-of-meaning and Swidler's toolkit; ask the room which better captures a homecoming, and why the difference matters.
22–36 Reading the step. The published summary of Center de Stage states that homecoming centers on a headlining performance; the original's step is documented in the reference card. Ask: is the step something Hillman has or something Hillman does?
36–50 Meaning under tension. The published summary states that Dwayne and Whitley return with visible tensions in their marriage. Ask what a homecoming performance means when the family it celebrates is strained — a test for both theories.
50–63 Swidler vs. Geertz, adjudicated. Half the room defends Geertz, half Swidler, using only the two documented scenes. Push each side to state what the other explains better.
63–75 Close and assign. Restate the big question; assign Homework 1; flag that Module 2 will take the same homecoming through Durkheim's theory of ritual.

Discussion questions

  1. Is a step routine better described as a web of shared meaning or as a tool a group picks up? What hangs on the choice?
  2. The published summary says the marriage is strained. Does that tension disrupt the homecoming's meaning, or is tension itself part of the culture on display?
  3. What would have to be true about a group for "culture as possession" to be the better model? Where does that model break?
  4. If culture is a toolkit, who stocks the toolkit, and who is left without the right tools? Is that a cultural question or a structural one?
  5. Name one thing the two scenes (1988 and 2026) share as culture despite being forty years apart.

Instructor notes

If the room collapses into "culture is everything." Accept it and then tax it: if everything is culture, the concept does no work. Force a negative case — what is not culture in these scenes — and use it to sharpen the definition.

If a student treats "what the step means" as a documented fact. Pause and apply the tiers: the step's existence and context are documented; its meaning is interpretation. Have the speaker re-state the claim with "I read this as…" and offer evidence from the summary or the card.
