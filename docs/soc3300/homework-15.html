Homework 15 — The capstone: a defended reading

Due: the final session (Module 15) · Length: 3–4 pages · Weight: the capstone (15% of the course grade)

Task

This is the capstone: a defended cultural analysis of a single text, read with the course's toolkit.

  1. Choose one text. One episode, one scene (as documented in the published summary or a reference card), or one run of reference-site cards. State it precisely — which episode, which fact.
  2. State a plain claim. One sentence, arguable, specific, unmistakably yours. If you cannot say it in one sentence, it is not a claim yet.
  3. Read it with at least three course concepts (e.g., Hall's decoding positions, Bourdieu's distinction, Gray's double burden, Durkheim's effervescence, Boym's nostalgia, hooks's gaze). Each concept must do distinct work on your text — not three names for the same observation.
  4. Separate the tiers. Mark every documented fact as documented (title, air date, summary) and every reading of yours as interpretation. Keep them visibly apart throughout.
  5. Name an honest limit. One thing your text cannot show, about itself or about the world — named precisely, not hedged.
  6. Argue the stakes. Why your claim matters — what follows if you are right.

Sources

  • At least three course readings (from sources.md), cited for the work they do
  • The reference site: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/
  • The season-close and theme cards: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/ep10.html#ep10-season-close and https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/ep10.html#ep10-brandy-theme

Criteria (out of 10)

Points For
3 Plain claim — arguable, specific, yours, statable in one sentence
3 Concepts do distinct work — at least three, each revealing something the others cannot
2 Evidence discipline — documented facts and interpretation visibly separated throughout
1 Honest limit — a real limit named, not a hedge
1 Stakes argued — why it matters if you are right
