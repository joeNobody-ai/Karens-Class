Homework 7 — Whose image, whose burden

Due: start of Module 8 · Length: 1 page · Weight: counts in the homework average (lowest two dropped)

Task

  1. Restate Herman Gray's argument in your own words: the Black sitcom is not a window onto Black life but an argument about blackness, made under a specific set of conditions. Keep it to three sentences.
  2. Apply it to the case: A Different World began as a Cosby Show spin-off and was later chaired by Debbie Allen (both documented). Using those two documented facts, argue how the conditions of production shaped what the genre was asked to carry — and to whom.
  3. Then bring in Robin Means Coleman: the audience is not unanimous. In one paragraph, describe two different legitimate things two different viewers might want from the same episode, and what that does to any single claim about the show's "meaning."
  4. Close with one sentence naming the double burden this genre carried that its white counterparts rarely did.

Sources

  • Herman Gray, Watching Race: Television and the Struggle for "Blackness" (1995)
  • Robin R. Means Coleman, African American Viewers and the Black Situation Comedy (2000)
  • The Cosby-Origin card: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/ep01.html#ep01-cosby-origin
  • The reference site: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/

Criteria (out of 10)

Points For
4 Gray's "argument, not window" distinction is restated accurately and then used, not just recited
3 The two documented production facts are made to do real work in the argument
2 The audience is treated as divided (Coleman), not as one voice
1 Evidence discipline: documented facts and interpretation kept apart
