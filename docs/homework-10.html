Homework 10 — Follow the money

Due: start of Session 11 · Length: 2 pages · Weight: counts in the homework average

This is the term's hardest homework, and the most transferable skill in the course.

Task

  1. Find two current, citable figures about historically Black colleges and universities. Choose from: - federal or state funding, appropriations, or land-grant funding gaps, or - enrolment, completion rates, or share of Black students enrolled in HBCUs. Use a primary source: a federal statistical agency, a federal or state education agency, a peer-reviewed study, or a national institution whose methodology is published.
  2. For each figure, report: the figure; the source (agency, report/table, year); the URL; the date you retrieved it; the denominator or comparison group; and one thing the figure cannot tell us.
  3. The mechanism. In 300 words, explain how the pattern your figures show is produced. Name mechanisms, not moral failings: appropriations formulas, duplicative programme rules, endowment differences, tuition dependence, state funding per student, demographic shifts.
  4. Apply it. Return to 2026 Episode 9 and the original S2E41 (Take This Job and Love It). Which of your mechanisms is visible in each text, and which is invisible?
  5. One sentence. State what a student movement could and could not change about Hillman's budget, given your mechanisms.

Sources

  • Ep 9 callback cards: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/ep09.html
  • Original: S2E41 Take This Job and Love It (1989-03-16)
  • Victor Ray, "A Theory of Racialized Organizations" (2019) — for organisations distributing resources and legitimacy along racial lines without intent
  • Primary-source starting points (HTTP-checked at build; verify before use): the National Center for Education Statistics (https://nces.ed.gov/) and its College Navigator (https://nces.ed.gov/collegenavigator/), and the Association of Public and Land-grant Universities (https://www.aplu.org/). The US Department of Education's HBCU programme pages are also worth using — they are at sites.ed.gov/whhbcu/, but they refuse automated checks, so open them in a browser and confirm before you put them on a handout.

Criteria (out of 10)

Points For
3 Two figures from primary sources, correctly described, with retrieval dates
3 Denominators stated, and the "cannot tell us" line is genuinely about measurement
3 Mechanisms are named as mechanisms and connect to the episodes
1 The closing sentence distinguishes what students can move from what they cannot

Do not cite an advocacy organisation's summary as your primary source. You may cite one in addition, clearly labelled as interpretation. Numbers in this assignment must be traceable to the table they came from.
