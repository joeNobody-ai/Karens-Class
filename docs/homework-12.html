Homework 12 — Three readings of one scene

Due: start of Session 13 · Length: 2 pages · Weight: counts in the homework average

Task

Using Stuart Hall's model, produce three readings of a single scene from the 2026 revival. Choose your scene, and choose one that is doing more than it appears to.

  1. The preferred reading. In 250 words: what does the scene most obviously want its audience to think or feel? Support it with the text's own signals — framing, music, who gets the last word.
  2. A negotiated reading. In 250 words: what would an audience that accepts the show's world but not this moment take from it? What do they keep, and what do they reject?
  3. An oppositional reading. In 250 words: what would an audience that rejects the scene's premise see in it? Remember Hall: an oppositional reading is not a complaint — it is a reading, and it has to work from the text.
  4. Which audience is the show built for? In 200 words, answer for the revival and for the original (S1E16, The Show Must Go On, or any original episode you know well). Use production facts if you can — episode count, release pattern, network or platform.
  5. One sentence. Which of your three readings do you find most convincing, and why is that not necessarily the one you prefer?

Sources

  • Stuart Hall, "Encoding/Decoding" (1973)
  • Herman Gray, Watching Race (1995), introduction
  • Robin R. Means Coleman, African American Viewers and the Black Situation Comedy (1998), ch. 1
  • Marlon Riggs, Color Adjustment (1992) — the film screened in Session 12
  • Callback cards, for production and casting facts: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/ep01.html#ep01-cosby-origin

Criteria (out of 10)

Points For
4 All three readings are readings — each works from evidence in the text
3 The oppositional reading is serious rather than a complaint
2 The audience argument uses production or distribution facts
1 The closing sentence distinguishes conviction from preference

Frequent error: making the oppositional reading something no one could hold while watching. If you cannot imagine a person who holds it, sharpen it until you can.
