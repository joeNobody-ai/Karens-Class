Homework 8 — The criteria of judgment

Due: start of Session 9 · Length: 1.5 pages · Weight: counts in the homework average

Task

A pageant is a machine for producing judgments. Your task is to take the machine apart.

  1. Extract the criteria. From 2026 Episode 7's pageant (Miss Gilbert Hall) and original S1E17 (Mr. Hillman, 1988-02-25), list every criterion of evaluation you can identify or reasonably infer: appearance, poise, lineage, talent, service, voice, family name. Mark which are stated in the material and which you are inferring.
  2. Who judges? For each text, name who holds the power to evaluate. Then say what the judges themselves are accountable for.
  3. The consequence for one character. Take Whitley in 1988: she enters to win her mother's approval. Write 200 words on what the pageant is for in her social world — not psychologically, socially.
  4. One documented consequence of colourism. Find one citation (a peer-reviewed article or a research-based book chapter) documenting a measurable consequence of colourism or appearance-based stratification. Report author, year, the finding, and the population it was measured in.
  5. One sentence. Does the pageant protect or discipline the people who compete in it? Answer, and name the evidence that decided you.

Sources

  • Pageant callback cards: https://joenobody-ai.github.io/different-world-notes/v2/ep07.html#ep07-miss-gilbert-hall
  • Original: S1E17 Mr. Hillman (1988-02-25)
  • Margaret L. Hunter, Race, Gender, and the Politics of Skin Tone (2005)
  • Evelyn Brooks Higginbotham on the politics of respectability (1993) — for the concept, from Righteous Discontent

Criteria (out of 10)

Points For
3 Criteria list distinguishes what is stated from what you inferred
2 Judges' own accountability is analysed, not just named
2 The Whitley paragraph is social analysis rather than character assessment
2 The colourism citation is real, correctly reported, and includes the population measured
1 The closing sentence commits to a claim

Hard line: no claims about anyone's appearance in this room, including your own. The subject is a mechanism, not a measurement of people present.
