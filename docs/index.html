Sociology · 15 sessions · upper division

A Different World: Black Experience and American Society

SOC 4150 · 15 sessions · 75 minutes · renumber to fit your catalogue.

Every session pairs a 2026 Netflix episode with the 1987–1993 original episode it calls back to, and asks what changed and what did not. The show's own device — the callback — is the course's method. Black experience is not a specialty bolted onto "general" sociology; it is where the general theory gets tested.

The 15 sessions

Session 1

What A Different World Is: Hillman as a Sociological Object

What kind of social object is a television series, and what does it take to read one sociologically?

Session 2

Voice, Censorship, and the Student as Political Actor

When a student speaks and an institution answers, what is being negotiated?

Session 3

Homecoming, Ritual, and Collective Identity

What does ritual do for a community, and what does it hide?

Session 4

Intimacy, Gender, and the Script of Romance

Is romance personal or structural — and what changes when both are true?

Session 5

Crisis, Care, and the Sociology of Health

Why did a Black sitcom put an HIV diagnosis on prime time in 1991, and what does a crisis mean on television now?

Session 6

Generation, Memory, and 'the Aunties'

How is a generation made, and who does the work of holding one together?

Session 7

Diaspora: Who Belongs to the 'Black Experience'?

What is at stake when a category that looks unified is contested from inside?

Session 8

Pageants, Colourism, and Hierarchy Inside Black Space

How does stratification work inside a group that is itself stratified against?

Session 9

Storm, Confinement, and the Conflict We Avoid

What does forced proximity do to a relationship, and what does the scene reveal about disclosure?

Session 10

Budgets, Institutions, and Racialized Organizations

What is an institution for, and what happens when the money runs out?

Session 11

The Finale as Argument: What Survives

What is a season finale arguing about an institution's future?

Session 12

Media, Image, and the Politics of Representation

Who controls the image, and what does an image do to the people it depicts?

Session 13

Family, Class, and the 'Culture' Question

When inequality is explained by culture, what is being explained — and what is being excused?

Session 14

The State, the Street, and the Ledger

How did a family sitcom put an uprising in its season premiere — and where is that tradition now?

Session 15

Capstone: The Black Experience Is American Society

If the case is American, what follows for how the discipline tells its story?

How to use this site

Students: start with the syllabus, then Session 1. Homework is due at the start of the following session and each assignment says exactly what to hand in and how it is marked.

Instructors: read the instructor guide before Session 1. Exam keys are in this repository under course/exams/ and, if built, at instructor/index.html — deliberately not linked from the navigation.

Evidence rule

The whole course runs on keeping three things apart: documented facts (episode titles, dates, credits, published summaries), interpretations (readings of what a scene or season argues), and hypotheses (claims on the table for testing). Students are graded on it from week one. The reference site does the same thing, which is why homework points there.
